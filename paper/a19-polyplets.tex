\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A \emph{polyplet} (also \emph{king-move animal}) is a finite set of unit cells of
the square lattice connected under king moves---two cells are adjacent if they
share an edge \emph{or} a corner. OEIS sequence
\href{https://oeis.org/A006770}{A006770} counts the \emph{fixed}
(translation-distinct) polyplets of $n$ cells and was, until now, known through
$n=18$. We report
\[
  a(19) = \num{151609203011580},
\]
computed from scratch and confirmed by two algorithms that share no counting
logic: direct Redelmeier enumeration, run as two decorrelated campaigns on
different architectures and compilers, and a column transfer-matrix count over
boundary-connectivity signatures. The two agree exactly and both reproduce all
eighteen previously published terms. We also give the free and one-sided
companions, $\mathrm{Free}(19)=\num{18951156321090}$ (the next term of A030222)
and $\mathrm{OneSided}(19)=\num{37902303297525}$ (not previously recorded), and a
battery of cross-checks. The contribution is the verified value, not the
methods, which are standard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The enumeration of lattice animals---finite connected sets of cells on a regular
lattice---is a classical problem at the meeting point of combinatorics and
statistical physics~\cite{klarner,jensen2001}. On the square lattice the two
standard families are \emph{polyominoes}, connected through shared edges, and
\emph{polyplets}, connected through shared edges or corners. The fixed counts grow
exponentially, so exact values are known only for modest $n$ and each new term is
a small computational milestone. The fixed-polyomino sequence
(\href{https://oeis.org/A001168}{A001168}) has been pushed
furthest~\cite{redelmeier,jensen2003,barequet}; the fixed-polyplet sequence
A006770 had been recorded through $n=18$.

This note reports and confirms the next term, $a(19)$. It is an amateur
contribution carried out with substantial AI assistance (Section~\ref{sec:ai});
its value lies not in algorithmic novelty but in an independent, cross-checked
computation of a previously unrecorded term. Since a single $15$-digit count from
one program is properly a conjecture, the value is computed twice, by two
algorithms that share no counting logic, on different hardware and compilers, and
both reproduce the entire published range.

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:object}

Fix the square lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and call its unit squares \emph{cells}. A
\emph{lattice animal} is a finite, nonempty, adjacency-connected set of cells.
Under \textbf{rook} (edge) adjacency the animals are \emph{polyominoes}; under
\textbf{king} (edge-or-corner) adjacency they are \emph{polyplets}. Every
polyomino is a polyplet but not conversely: two cells touching only at a corner
form a polyplet and not a polyomino (Figure~\ref{fig:specimen}). One subtlety: a
polyplet connected \emph{purely} by diagonals lives on a single checkerboard
colour class, which under diagonal adjacency is isomorphic (rotate $45^\circ$,
rescale by $\sqrt2$) to the square lattice---so such a shape is a polyomino in
disguise. A genuinely king shape mixes edge and corner contacts.

Animals are counted \textbf{fixed} (up to translation; rotations and reflections
distinct), \textbf{one-sided} (up to translation and rotation), or \textbf{free}
(up to all isometries). A006770 is the fixed count. The cleanest illustration is
$a(2)=4$: the horizontal domino, the vertical domino, and the \emph{two} diagonal
dominoes, which are mirror images and counted separately. (The free count is
\href{https://oeis.org/A030222}{A030222}, $\mathrm{Free}(2)=2$.)

\label{sec:result}

\paragraph{Companions.}
The same cells counted under coarser equivalences give
$\mathrm{Free}(19)=\num{18951156321090}$ (the next term of A030222) and
$\mathrm{OneSided}(19)=\num{37902303297525}$ (no OEIS sequence exists, so that
column is entirely new). These follow from the fixed count by Burnside's lemma
over the square's symmetry group:
$\mathrm{Free}=\tfrac18(\mathrm{Fixed}+2R_{90}+R_{180}+2H+2D)$ and
$\mathrm{OneSided}=\tfrac14(\mathrm{Fixed}+2R_{90}+R_{180})$, where the symmetric
sub-counts are computed by a dedicated enumerator over fundamental domains (here
$R_{90}=0$ since a $90^\circ$-symmetric animal needs $n\equiv 0,1\pmod 4$ while
$19\equiv3$; $R_{180}=\num{10178520}$, axis $=\num{9765524}$, diagonal
$=\num{8923786}$). The assembled free counts reproduce A030222 exactly over its
entire published range ($n\le17$), and an independent Python reimplementation
matches the C++ symmetric counts at $n=19$.

\section{Method A: direct enumeration}
\label{sec:methodA}

Redelmeier's backtracking enumeration~\cite{redelmeier}, adapted to king
adjacency, grows every animal exactly once from an anchored cell: it maintains an
ordered frontier of \emph{untried} neighbours and recurses, with the standard
rule that a cell once placed or rejected at a level is excluded from deeper
levels. The recursion tallies by size; no animal is stored. Being embarrassingly
parallel by sub-tree, the count was run as two decorrelated campaigns---on
\texttt{gympie} (Apple silicon, Apple~clang, frontier split $7\times64$) and
\texttt{ayr} (AMD Threadripper, GCC/x86, split $8\times96$). The per-size outputs
are \textbf{byte-identical} (SHA-256 \texttt{558f0bdf\ldots}). Because the
arithmetic is exact integer addition, a shared hardware/compiler/scheduling fault
could not produce identical \emph{wrong} digits; the one error mode the campaigns
cannot jointly catch is a bug in the shared \emph{algorithm}, which is what
Method~B rules out.

\section{Method B: the column transfer matrix}
\label{sec:methodB}

The second method counts the same animals by an entirely different principle:
dynamic programming over the connectivity of a moving boundary, with no animal
materialised---a king adaptation of the transfer-matrix / finite-lattice
technique for polyominoes~\cite{jensen2001,jensen2003,bousquet}. Every animal has
a bounding-box height $H$, and animals of height exactly $H$ are counted
separately, so $a(n)=\sum_{H=1}^{n}\byH(n)$ (Table~\ref{tab:byheight}); the
heights are independent, checkpointable sub-problems.

Within a height, confine the animal to an $H$-row strip and sweep columns left to
right. A \emph{boundary signature}---the occupancy of the most recent column, a
partition of its filled cells into the partial animal's connected components, and
two flags for whether rows $0$ and $H-1$ have yet been touched---is a Markov state,
since a future cell attaches only to the current column or later. Because king
adjacency reaches the diagonal neighbours $(c{-}1,r{\pm}1)$, the transfer moves an
\emph{entire column} at once, keeping the previous column wholly present;
connectivity across the step is resolved by union--find. Three invariants make
accepted sweeps biject with fixed polyplets: the first column is column $0$ (no
horizontal double-counting); an animal is tallied only with both touch-flags set
(height exactly $H$); and only when its boundary is a single component, partial
states that can never reconnect being killed. A size-budget prune drops any state
whose cells-so-far plus an \emph{admissible} lower bound on cells-still-needed
exceeds $n$; since the bound never over-estimates, the prune can only make a count
come out low, so reproducing every published term certifies that it dropped no
animal in range. The hot path uses a flat open-addressing map keyed by a
fixed-width signature, a stack union--find, and a viable-mask generator; heights
were run as independent checkpointed jobs.

\section{Confirmation}
\label{sec:confirm}

The value rests on several independent checks.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{External truth and Method A $=$ Method B.} Both methods reproduce
        A006770 exactly for every $n\le 18$, and the transfer-matrix totals
        $\sum_H\byH(n)$ are byte-identical to the Redelmeier output (same SHA-256)
        at every term through $n=19$.
  \item \textbf{Forward vs.\ backward DP.} A separate backward completion-count
        DP, run on a different host, compiler, and ISA, reproduced a
        representative height term $B_{17}(19)=\num{47839787379}$ identically.
  \item \textbf{Structural cross-checks.} The transfer matrix's
        (height,\,size) and (size,\,perimeter) marginals match the enumeration's
        through $n=12$; of the generated polyplets, those connected under rook,
        and (equally) under bishop, adjacency reproduce A001168 (the $45^\circ$
        colour-class invariant), as does the perimeter-$4n$ slice.
  \item \textbf{Fault injection.} Planted errors are caught (a dropped diagonal at
        $n=2$, a doubled count at $n=1$, a prune off-by-one at $n=10$) while a
        provably count-preserving change survives---so the checks fail on wrong
        programs and pass on right ones.
  \item \textbf{Hygiene.} Both engines are AddressSanitizer/UBSan clean; the
        multithreaded transfer matrix is ThreadSanitizer clean and bit-identical
        to the serial path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Computational particulars and further results}
\label{sec:runs}

Method~A completed within hours per campaign. Method~B is dominated not by the
tallest strips but by those with a few cells of vertical slack $n-H$, where the
signature space is largest; on \texttt{ayr} (32 cores, 78\,GB) the production run
went height-by-height with $H=17,18,19$ the long poles, peak memory in the low
tens of gigabytes. A full provenance ledger (host, git commit, binary/result
SHA-256) accompanies the runs.

The counting DP is also an exact uniform sampler~\cite{nijenhuis}: walking the
transfer matrix forward, choosing each next column with probability proportional
to the completions it admits, emits a uniform random animal in one pass; an
independent king-BFS verifier confirmed soundness on every specimen, and the
height histogram matched $\byH(19)/a(19)$. A $10{,}000$-specimen sample shows the
typical $19$-cell polyplet is \emph{sparse, branchy, corner-glued}: about ten
rook-connected pieces held together mostly by corners ($59\%$ of contacts),
$\sim\!25\%$ box density, roughly a $28\%$ chance of enclosing a hole, and
essentially never an honest polyomino (probability
$A001168(19)/a(19)\approx4\times10^{-5}$). As a by-product the enumeration also
yields new hole-stratified sub-sequences (hole-free fixed polyplets
$1,4,20,109,622,\dots$ under the topologically consistent $4$-connected-background
convention, validated to $n=14$ and absent from OEIS). These and the growth
estimate are detailed in the repository; $a(20)$ is within reach of the same
machinery.

\section{Data and code availability}
\label{sec:availability}

The source (both engines, the sampler, the test gates), b-files for
$n=1,\dots,19$ of A006770 and A030222, the per-height rows $\byH(19)$, the sample
and its statistics, the hole sub-sequences, and a JSON provenance ledger are in
the accompanying repository. The cross-checks are encoded as runnable gates and a
self-contained checker re-verifies every numerical claim in this paper, so a
reader can regenerate the b-files and reproduce the results with the included
harness.
