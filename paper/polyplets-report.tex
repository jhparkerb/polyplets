\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A \emph{polyplet} (king-move animal) is a finite set of unit cells of the square
lattice connected through shared edges or corners. OEIS sequence
\href{https://oeis.org/A006770}{A006770} counts the \emph{fixed} polyplets of $n$
cells, previously known through $n=18$. We report twenty-two new terms, through
\[
  a(40) = \num{56749893611764175164545926946127},
\]
each computed from scratch and reproducing every prior term. Every number
carries an explicit confidence tier: $a(1)$--$a(22)$ are confirmed by two
algorithms sharing no counting logic (direct enumeration and the transfer
matrix); $a(23)$--$a(38)$ by a single algorithm family, cross-checked per
term against \emph{held-out} closed forms for the top diagonals of the
height triangle; $a(39)$ and $a(40)$ lack that held-out check on their top
swept stratum---the sequence ends here, so it will never exist---which we
grade rather than paper over. The one-sided companion A030233 is extended to
$n=34$, and the free, bilateral, and asymmetric companions (A030222,
A030234, A030235, A194596) to $n=32$.

The machinery also yields structural results. The diagonals of the height
triangle obey an exponential/cumulant law whose shape---a polynomial of
degree $k$ times $3^{\,n-1-3k}$, with its onset and integrality---is
\emph{proved} here by an exact walk-plus-clusters decomposition, and is
lattice-universal: the same law, and the same mod-$p$ ``spine cubic''
$W^3=W^2+t$, govern polyominoes and polyhexes, with the prime set by the
lattice's drift count. The diagonals of the diagonal-mirror symmetry
triangle obey a period-two quasi-polynomial law, likewise proved in shape.
The by-height generating function $\sum T(n,H)x^ny^H$ is \emph{not
D-finite}, by a Northcott-style argument on the strip growth constants that
is lighter than the existing haruspicy route and transports to polyominoes,
polyhexes, and polyiamonds. We bracket the growth constant rigorously,
$6.543\le\lambda\le9.3153$, both ends by machine-checkable exact
certificates, the upper bound apparently the first recorded for this
lattice (the numerical estimate is $\lambda\approx7.111$). Finally, A006770
is stratified by hole count through $n=18$, and the largest area a single
hole can enclose is proved to be exactly
$\lfloor(n-2)^2/8+\tfrac12\rfloor$. Results computed and validated but not
proved are labeled as such; all code, data, and a claim-verifier are
released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The enumeration of lattice animals---finite connected sets of cells on a regular
lattice---is a classical problem at the meeting point of combinatorics and
statistical physics~\cite{klarner,jensen2001}. On the square lattice the two
standard families are \emph{polyominoes}, connected through shared edges, and
\emph{polyplets}, connected through shared edges or corners. The fixed counts
grow exponentially, so exact values are known only for modest $n$ and each new
term is a small computational milestone. The fixed-polyomino sequence
(\href{https://oeis.org/A001168}{A001168}) has been pushed
furthest~\cite{redelmeier,jensen2003,barequet}; the fixed-polyplet sequence
A006770 had been recorded through $n=18$.

This document is a \emph{computational report}: its claims are numbers, the
provenance and verification of those numbers, and the empirical structure
found in them. It reports the terms $a(19)$ through $a(40)$ and the results
that grew out of computing them, including the diagonal law of the height
triangle---whose shape is proved here via an exact walk-plus-clusters
decomposition---its mod-3 algebraic structure, and the diagonal-mirror
triangle's quasi-polynomial law, also proved in shape
(Section~\ref{sec:diagonals}). It is an
amateur contribution carried out with substantial AI assistance
(Section~\ref{sec:ai}). The data appears in the document as well as the
repository: a report's job is to be usable by the next person to attempt
these computations.

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:object}

Fix the square lattice $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and call its unit squares \emph{cells}. A
\emph{lattice animal} is a finite, nonempty, adjacency-connected set of cells.
Under \textbf{rook} (edge) adjacency the animals are \emph{polyominoes}; under
\textbf{king} (edge-or-corner) adjacency they are \emph{polyplets}. Every
polyomino is a polyplet but not conversely: two cells touching only at a corner
form a polyplet and not a polyomino. One subtlety: a
polyplet connected \emph{purely} by diagonals lives on a single checkerboard
colour class, itself a square lattice, so such a shape is a polyomino in
disguise; a genuinely king shape mixes edge and corner contacts. (The
remark is illustrative only---the companion arithmetic of
Section~\ref{sec:companions} works on the full king-move class under $D_4$
and never decomposes by colour class.)

Animals are counted \textbf{fixed} (up to translation; rotations and
reflections distinct), \textbf{one-sided} (up to translation and rotation), or
\textbf{free} (up to all isometries). A006770 is the fixed count. Two
triangles refine it: the \emph{height triangle} $T(n,H)$ (animals of $n$ cells
and bounding-box height exactly $H$, so $a(n)=\sum_H T(n,H)$), and, for each
symmetry $g$ of the square, the count of fixed animals invariant under $g$,
which enters the companion counts by Burnside's lemma
(Section~\ref{sec:companions}).

\paragraph{Growth.}
The successive ratios $r_n=a(n)/a(n{-}1)$ climb slowly:
$r_{20}\approx6.765$, $r_{27}\approx6.853$, $r_{34}\approx6.905$,
$r_{40}\approx6.935$. Fitting
the two-parameter ansatz $r_n=\lambda(1+\theta/n)$ by least squares over
trailing windows gives $\lambda=7.102$, $7.104$, $7.106$, $7.107$ (with
$\theta$ between $-0.95$ and $-0.96$) for windows starting at $n=10$, $15$,
$20$, $25$ and ending at $40$; the upward drift with window start is the
signature of an unmodelled confluent correction. Refitting with a confluent
term, $r_n=\lambda(1+\theta/n+c/n^{1+\Delta_1})$, with $\Delta_1$ scanned on
a grid, the exponent $\Delta_1=\tfrac12$ gives the smallest residuals on
every window (by factors from ${\sim}4$ on the shortest window to
${\sim}140$ on the longest) and removes the drift:
$\lambda=7.1108$--$7.1111$ across the same
four windows, with $\theta$ between $-1.02$ and $-1.03$---consistent with
the universal two-dimensional lattice-animal value
$\theta=-1$~\cite{jensen2001}, which the fit was free to miss. An
independent first-order differential-approximant analysis of the series
(calibrated to recover $\lambda$ and $\theta$ exactly from synthetic
$n^\theta\lambda^n$ data, and stable across rescaling and the number of
trailing terms used) locates the dominant singularity at
$\lambda=7.110$ with exponent $\theta=-1.000$, corroborating both the
estimate and the universal $\theta=-1$ by a route independent of the
ratio fit. We accordingly estimate $\lambda\approx7.111$; this is an
estimate, not a rigorous bound---the rigorous two-sided bracket follows.

\paragraph{A rigorous lower bound on $\lambda$.}
Polyplets of bounding-box height at most $H$ are a subfamily of all
polyplets, so their growth constant $\mu_H$---the dominant eigenvalue of the
height-$\le H$ strip transfer operator $M(x)$, whose entries are powers of
$x$ counting cells placed---satisfies $\mu_H\le\lambda$ for every $H$, and
$\mu_H$ increases with $H$. Each rung is certified rather than measured. Since
$\rho(M(x))$ is increasing in $x$ and equals $1$ at $x=1/\mu_H$, exhibiting a
nonnegative integer vector $v\ne0$ and a rational $x$ with $M(x)v\ge v$
componentwise proves $\rho(M(x))\ge1$, hence $\mu_H\ge1/x$; the lower half of
the Collatz--Wielandt formula needs neither positivity of $v$ nor
irreducibility of $M$~\cite{hornjohnson}. The witness is a finite object and
the check is exact integer arithmetic, so a reader re-verifies the bound
without rerunning the search that found it. Carrying the ladder to $H=17$
($6{,}536{,}381$ states) gives
\[
  \lambda \;\ge\; \mu_{17} \;\ge\; 6.543 ,
\]
with certificates at every rung from the independent anchor
$\mu_2=1+\sqrt2$ upward ($\mu_{11}\ge5.9916957$, $\mu_{15}\ge6.443532$,
$\mu_{16}\ge6.4984$). The ladder converges to $\lambda$ from below but
slowly---the rungs gain about $0.05$ each while cost grows ${\sim}3.5\times$
per rung, reaching $11.8$\,GB at $H=17$---so it is stopped here rather than
exhausted.

A weaker but closed-form bound comes from a solvable subclass: the
\emph{directed} king animals of Bacher~\cite{bacher} (OEIS A047781), counted
by $\tfrac14\!\left((1+t)/\sqrt{1-6t+t^{2}}-1\right)$, whose dominant
singularity at $t=3-2\sqrt2$ gives $\lambda\ge3+2\sqrt2\approx5.828$ with no
computation at all; Bacher's multi-directed subclass sharpens this to
$\lambda\gtrsim6.475$ numerically (Corollary~12). Both are superseded by the
certified ladder and are recorded here only as the closed-form comparison.

\paragraph{A rigorous upper bound on $\lambda$.}
No upper bound on $\lambda$ appears in the literature. We obtain one by a
Bui-style finite-type convolution certificate~\cite{bui2025}: mark a cell of the
animal, case on the occupancy of a single neighbour at a time, and record the
resulting local patterns as a finite set of ``types'' whose generating functions
satisfy a system of polynomial convolution inequalities
$\varphi_T \le \varphi_{T'} + \varphi_{T'}\varphi_D$. A positive rational vector
$u$ with $u_T \ge F_x(u)_T$ for every type---checked in exact rational
arithmetic---proves the system bounded at $x$, hence $\lambda \le 1/x$. With a
split window of radius $3$ (a $5930$-type system) the certificate $x=2147/20000$
gives
\[
  \lambda \;\le\; \tfrac{20000}{2147} \;\approx\; 9.3153 ,
\]
verified with no floating point in the deductive chain. Together with the
certified strip ladder this brackets the growth constant two-sided,
$6.543 \le \lambda \le 20000/2147$, i.e.\ $6.543 \le \lambda \le 9.3153$,
consistent with the numerical estimate $\lambda\approx7.111$. Both ends are
machine-checkable in exact arithmetic. The gap reflects a
structural limit: the convolution over-count grows with animal size (distant
overlaps between the two factors), so finite-type methods of this class floor
strictly above $\lambda$; the crude decision-tree bound $\lambda\le5^5/4^4\approx
12.2$ is superseded.

\section{Methods: an escalation ladder}
\label{sec:methods}

Four counting methods (A--D) computed these numbers, and a fifth (E)
replaced their most expensive cells with closed forms; each subsection
states what ran out and
what replaced it. All engines share three disciplines: exact integer
arithmetic; every production run re-derives \emph{all} smaller terms, so a
regression anywhere in range is visible; and admissible pruning only (a prune
may only ever \emph{lose} animals, so reproducing the published range
certifies that it lost none).

\subsection{Method A: direct enumeration (to $n=22$)}
\label{sec:methodA}

Redelmeier's backtracking enumeration~\cite{redelmeier}, adapted to king
adjacency, grows every animal exactly once from an anchored cell: it maintains
an ordered frontier of \emph{untried} neighbours and recurses, with the
standard rule that a cell once placed or rejected at a level is excluded from
deeper levels. The recursion tallies by size; no animal is stored. Being
embarrassingly parallel by sub-tree, the count was run as two decorrelated
campaigns---on Apple silicon (Apple~clang, frontier split $7\times64$) and
AMD/x86 (GCC, split $8\times96$). The per-size outputs are
\textbf{byte-identical}. Because the two campaigns differ in hardware,
compiler, and scheduling and the arithmetic is exact integer addition, a
transient or platform-specific fault in one is overwhelmingly unlikely to be
mirrored digit-for-digit in the other; the error mode they cannot jointly
catch is a bug in the shared \emph{algorithm}, which is what the transfer
matrix (Methods~B,~C) rules out by counting the same animals a completely
different way. An optimized kernel (the terminal frontier level counted in
bulk, compile-time neighbour offsets; ${\approx}2\times$ faster) run as a
three-architecture fleet (ARM, x86, Apple silicon) extended the whole-row
enumeration to $n=22$---byte-identical across machines and matching the
transfer-matrix totals term for term. Direct enumeration and the transfer
matrix are thus two independent algorithms agreeing through $a(22)$, which
is the \tierone{} anchor.

\subsection{Method B: the column transfer matrix (to $n\approx23$)}
\label{sec:methodB}

The second method counts the same animals by an entirely different principle:
dynamic programming over the connectivity of a moving boundary, with no animal
materialised---a king adaptation of the transfer-matrix / finite-lattice
technique for polyominoes~\cite{jensen2001,jensen2003,bousquet}. Animals of
bounding-box height exactly $H$ are counted separately, so
$a(n)=\sum_{H=1}^{n}T(n,H)$; the heights are independent, checkpointable
sub-problems.

Within a height, confine the animal to an $H$-row strip and sweep columns left
to right. A \emph{boundary signature}---the occupancy of the most recent
column, a partition of its filled cells into the partial animal's connected
components, and two flags for whether rows $0$ and $H-1$ have yet been
touched---is a Markov state, since a future cell attaches only to the current
column or later. Because king adjacency reaches the diagonal neighbours
$(c{-}1,r{\pm}1)$, the transfer moves an \emph{entire column} at once, keeping
the previous column wholly present; connectivity across the step is resolved
by union--find. Three invariants make accepted sweeps biject with fixed
polyplets: the first column is column $0$ (no horizontal double-counting); an
animal is tallied only with both touch-flags set (height exactly $H$); and
only when its boundary is a single component---partial states that can never
reconnect are pruned. A size-budget prune drops any state whose cells-so-far
plus an \emph{admissible} lower bound on cells-still-needed exceeds $n$.

Concretely, a state is a fixed-width byte string: one byte per strip row
holding the cell's connected-component label ($0$ for empty), with labels
\emph{canonically renumbered in first-occurrence order} after every
transition---the normalization that prevents equivalent partitions from
proliferating as distinct states---plus the two touch flags; the value
attached to a state is its vector of counts by size. States live in a flat
open-addressing hash map keyed by that string (about $300$ bytes per state
including counts at production sizes). The full layout is in the release's
\texttt{core/signature.h}.

Method B confirmed $a(19)$ against Method A (byte-identical totals) and
carried the sequence into the early twenties before its state space---which
keeps a whole column of connectivity alive per step---outgrew memory.

\subsection{Method C: the cell-at-a-time transfer matrix (to $n=40$)}
\label{sec:methodC}

The production engine for $a(24)$--$a(40)$ replaces the column step with a
\emph{cell-at-a-time} step: the boundary advances one cell per transition,
so the frontier is a staircase---$r$ cells of the new column above $H-r$
cells of the old---plus one retained north-west \emph{carry} cell, kept
because king adjacency lets the next cell reach diagonally back across the
kink in the staircase; without it the step would sever connectivity
information the lattice still provides. The state encoding is as in
Method~B (canonical component-label bytes over the staircase cells and the
carry, touch flags, counts), and each transition decides a single cell,
resolving connectivity by union--find over its at-most-three placed
neighbours. The finer step multiplies the number of transitions but shrinks
the live state space and, decisively, the volume that must be reshuffled
between passes (measured speedups over Method B grow from $\sim$29$\times$
at $H=12$ to $\sim$200$\times$ at $H=16$). At frontier sizes beyond RAM the
state map spills to disk: the frontier is hash-sharded, expanded shard by
shard, and merged by external passes, so the binding resource is disk
bandwidth rather than memory. Heights remain independent jobs; per-height
results are banked with their provenance and re-summed by a combiner that
refuses partial or duplicated inputs.

\paragraph{I/O integrity.}
A multi-hour external shuffle is itself a failure surface, so the disk
layer is engineered to fail closed rather than lose states silently. Every
spill file carries a self-describing header (magic, format version, byte
order, key width, and the \emph{record count}, so truncation---including
the short writes produced by resource exhaustion---is detected on read
rather than absorbed); readers and the merge refuse malformed or
count-mismatched inputs; the per-height combiner refuses missing, empty,
or duplicated shards and takes a lock against concurrent runs on the same
directory; and kept artifacts are listed in a manifest with per-file
checksums. These refusal paths are not assumed to work: the release's gate
suite fault-injects each failure mode (truncated file, missing shard,
duplicated rows, held lock) and requires the pipeline to abort loudly.

Each production run reproduces the complete triangle---every $T(n,H)$ for all
smaller $n$---so each new term arrives with a full-chain regression against
everything already known, including the \tierone{} range.

\paragraph{Which cells are computed and which are injected.}
The headline totals contain both, so the split must be stated exactly. For
$a(40)$, heights $3\le H\le21$ were swept for real ($95.9\%$ of the total);
$H=1,2$ are elementary closed forms; and $H\ge22$---the diagonals
$k\le18$---were \emph{injected} from the fitted closed forms of
Section~\ref{sec:heightdiag}, contributing $4.1\%$ of $a(40)$. The
analogous split holds for the preceding terms, the injection boundary
advancing as each new $P_k$ is certified (at $a(36)$: real sweep
$H\!=\!3$--$19$, injection $5.6\%$); the range $n\le19$ contains no
injected cells at all. The discipline governing injection is strict and
uniform: a $P_k$ is fitted on real-swept cells of earlier terms, must
reproduce every other real-swept cell on its diagonal (holdout), must then
predict the \emph{next} term's real-swept cell on that diagonal
exactly---the newest diagonal is always swept for real once more before its
formula is trusted---and only after surviving that live prediction is it
injected for subsequent terms. Section~\ref{sec:confirm} gives the
resulting holdout chain, and the one formula that never earned injection.
Readers who want totals free of any formula assistance may sum
Table~\ref{tab:byheight40}'s rows $H\le21$ against the banked per-height

\subsection{Method D: symmetric transfer matrices (the companions)}
\label{sec:methodD}

The companion counts need, for each symmetry $g$ of the square, the number of
fixed animals invariant under $g$. An animal fixed by $g$ is determined by a
fundamental-domain slice of itself, so these counts behave like
$\sqrt{a(n)}$-scale problems and can reach $n=34$ where the full count cannot
be re-derived independently. Two engines compute them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item An \emph{orbit-graph Redelmeier} enumerator (explicit, per-animal):
        growth over cell-orbits under $\langle g\rangle$. Being explicit it is
        the cross-algorithm oracle, and it carried all four symmetry types to
        $n=24$.
  \item A family of \emph{symmetric transfer matrices}, one per conjugacy
        class: a palindromic-column sweep for the axis mirror; a half-plane
        sweep with a self-gluing seam for the $180^\circ$ rotation (with a
        transpose restriction, counting each animal from its wide
        orientation, that collapses the tall strips); and, for the diagonal
        mirror, an exact-bounding-box \emph{hook} sweep---the frontier is the
        L-shaped shell of a growing square, swept corner outward, with the
        mirror symmetry enforced by construction. The $90^\circ$ count, whose
        invariant animals have $\sim n/4$ free cells, stays within the
        explicit enumerator's reach through $n=34$.
\end{itemize}

The two engines agree---live, in the release's test gate---for every type at
small $n$, and byte-identical at the $n=24$ oracle; beyond $n=24$ the
transfer-matrix values are single-algorithm (\tiertwo{}), validated by prefix
against the oracle range on every run. The diagonal-mirror sweep is the
expensive one (its bounding box is forced square, so the frontier compresses
least); its production form stores the frontier in flat sharded arrays with
ranged count vectors, and farms disjoint exact-bounding-box strips across
machines, the partial totals summing exactly by construction; the completed
$n=32$ farm (32 strips across two machines) prefix-matches both the
$n\le24$ oracle range and the earlier $n\le28$ run exactly.

\subsection{Method E: diagonal closed forms}
\label{sec:methodE}

The top diagonals of the height triangle---$T(n,n-k)$ for small $k$---are a
sweep's most expensive cells and its least populated. They have exact
closed forms (Section~\ref{sec:heightdiag}; the polynomial law is proved,
Theorem~\ref{thm:diaglaw}): for each $k\le18$ a polynomial, fitted from two
data points and then validated on held-out terms, replaces the top real
sweep height. Beyond the speedup they are per-term independent checks of
the production engine---a polynomial pinned on terms $\le n_0$ that
predicts a frontier cell of term $n_0{+}1$ exactly is evidence no
single-engine recount can provide, and that role survives the law being
proved, since a theorem about the triangle does not validate a program that
computes it. The same idea on the diagonal-mirror triangle yields the
quasi-polynomials that assist the $n=33$ companions (\tierthree{} when
used).

\section{Validation architecture}
\label{sec:confirm}

The verification burden shifts across the sequence, and the checks are
designed around the weakest link at each stage.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{External truth.} Every method reproduces A006770 exactly for
        every $n\le18$, on every production run (the triangle re-derives all
        smaller terms as a by-product).
  \item \textbf{Direct enumeration $=$ transfer matrix through $a(22)$.}
        Byte-identical totals from algorithmically disjoint counters: Method~A
        against Method~B at $a(19)$, and---after the kernel speedup and the
        three-architecture fleet run---direct whole-row enumeration against
        Method~C's totals through $a(22)$. This is the \tierone{} anchor.
  \item \textbf{Cross-architecture recounts.} $a(34)$'s full triangle was
        recomputed on a second architecture and compiler (x86/GCC against
        ARM/clang production) and is byte-identical, row by row; earlier
        terms had per-term recounts of the heaviest strata. Beyond $a(34)$
        the recount coverage weakens and we say so: $a(35)$'s second-machine
        pass covered only part of the height range; $a(36)$ was computed
        with its heights \emph{split} across two architectures (x86 for
        $H\le18$ and $H\ge20$, ARM for the $H=19$ long pole) rather than
        recomputed whole; and $a(37)$--$a(40)$ were computed on a single
        machine (ARM) with no second-architecture pass at all---their
        decorrelation rests on the full-chain reproduction and the held-out
        diagonal checks below. Two mitigations at the final term: the
        $H=20$ column of $a(40)$ was re-swept standalone from a fresh
        checkpoint and is byte-identical, and the $H=21$ sweep carries the
        nineteen-row closed-form cross-check described next.
  \item \textbf{Held-out diagonal closed forms.} Each $P_k$ was fitted on
        the smallest admissible terms and then required to reproduce every
        later term's cell $T(n,n-k)$ exactly. The chain runs the full
        ladder---$P_{15}\to T(33,18)$, $P_{16}\to T(35,19)$,
        $P_{17}\to T(37,20)$, $P_{18}\to T(39,21)$---each formula pinned
        before the term it went on to predict existed. A passed holdout
        retroactively certifies the top cells its formula was fitted on (a
        degree-$k$ polynomial through a wrong fit point does not predict
        the next term's cell exactly), which is what holds
        $a(33)$--$a(38)$ at \tiertwo{}. The strongest single check came
        free with the final term: the $a(40)$ run's real $H=21$ sweep
        reproduced the closed-form values $P_k(n)\,3^{n-1-3k}$ on every one
        of its rows $n=21$--$39$ simultaneously, re-confirming all nineteen
        wired levels at a fresh point each, in the very sweep that produced
        $T(40,21)$. The \tiertwominus{} grade attaches to $a(39)$ and
        $a(40)$: their top real cells $T(39,20)$ and $T(40,21)$ lie on
        diagonal $k=19$, and $P_{19}$---fitted from exactly these two
        cells---has no independent holdout, nor ever will, the sequence
        closing at $a(40)$. Those strata run the same code path the lower
        $P_k$ certified; what lacks certification is the top cells
        themselves, not the path.
  \item \textbf{Modular consistency (design capability, not a production
        gate).} A residue-counter/CRT-reconstruction path exists and is
        regression-tested (independent residue systems must agree, which
        would catch overflow and transient corruption---not shared
        enumeration bugs, which the items above target), but it has only
        been exercised at small $n$, not against any production frontier
        sweep $a(20)$--$a(40)$. Overflow protection for the reported terms
        instead comes from the items already listed above (cross-machine
        and cross-algorithm recount, u128-vs-u64 comparison at the sizes
        where both are feasible) plus the structural and fault-injection
        checks below.
  \item \textbf{Structural cross-checks.} The transfer matrices'
        (height,\,size) and (size,\,perimeter) marginals match the explicit
        enumeration's; rook- and bishop-connected sub-counts reproduce
        A001168; the $H$-cell height-$H$ diagonal is exactly $3^{H-1}$;
        Burnside numerators satisfy their $/4$ and $/8$ integrality at every
        term.
  \item \textbf{Fault injection.} Planted errors are caught (a dropped
        diagonal at $n=2$, a doubled count at $n=1$, a prune off-by-one at
        $n=10$) while a provably count-preserving change survives---the
        checks fail on wrong programs and pass on right ones.
  \item \textbf{Hygiene.} Every engine---the legacy oracles and the
        production map/merge workers (both the column and cell-at-a-time
        kernels)---is AddressSanitizer/UBSan clean at gate scale (small
        $n$, against fixtures; the sanitizer build compiles the same
        source the $-O3$ production binary does, unmodified); the
        multithreaded paths are bit-identical to serial; every kept result
        traces to a clean-tree commit stamp in a provenance ledger, and the
        cross-checks are encoded as gates that run before release.
\end{itemize}

\section{The diagonal structure of king-animal triangles}
\label{sec:diagonals}

Both triangles in this paper---the height triangle of the full count and the
exact-bounding-box triangle of the diagonal-mirror count---turn out to have
exactly solvable top diagonals. We record the laws, the fitting discipline,
and what is proved versus pinned.

\subsection{The height triangle: an exponential/cumulant law}
\label{sec:heightdiag}

A height-$H$ animal of exactly $H$ cells is one cell per row drifting by
$\{-1,0,+1\}$: $T(H,H)=3^{H-1}$. On the diagonal $k=n-H$ ($k$ cells above
minimum), the normalized counts
$P_k(n) = T(n,\,n-k)\,\cdot 3^{\,1+3k-n}$
satisfy
\[
  P_k(n) \;=\; [y^k]\,\exp\!\Big(\sum_{j\ge1}(a_j+b_j\,n)\,y^j\Big),
\]
an exponential of \emph{cumulants linear in $n$}: $b_j$ is the per-site
density of connected weight-$j$ defect clusters against the random-walk
background, $a_j$ the boundary correction---so each level costs exactly two
new constants, fitted from two data points and validated on all others. The
leading coefficient is $25^k/k!$ (twenty-five single-defect species per walk
site, an integer the fit must and does reproduce at every level), and $P_k$
times $k!$ must be integral. Levels $k\le18$ are fitted, validated, and wired
into production (Section~\ref{sec:methodE}); each was pinned on the earliest
admissible terms and holds on every later one. (A nineteenth level, $P_{19}$,
is determined by the final two top cells and passes the leading-coefficient
and integrality checks, but with the sequence closing at $a(40)$ it has no
holdout and is never used; we record it without certifying it.)

The law's shape is not an empirical regularity but a theorem:

\begin{theorem}[diagonal law]
\label{thm:diaglaw}
For every $k\ge0$ there is a polynomial $q_k$ of degree $\le k$ with
$T(H{+}k,\,H)=q_k(H)\,3^H$ for \emph{all} $H\ge k+1$---equivalently
$T(n,n{-}k)=P_k(n)\,3^{\,n-1-3k}$ for all $n\ge 2k+1$ with
$P_k(n)=3^{1+2k}q_k(n{-}k)$---and $P_k$ takes integer values at every
integer.
\end{theorem}

The proof is an exact decomposition. Reading an animal row by row, a
single-cell row is a cut vertex (a king step changes the row index by at
most one), so every animal splits uniquely into a drift walk decorated by
finitely many \emph{cluster} types (maximal runs of multi-cell rows), each
with a finite configuration count; this gives an exact rational identity
for $\sum_{n,H}T(n,H)\,y^{\,n-H}z^H$, verified cell-for-cell against the
computed triangle including all boundary corrections. Every cluster row
carries at least one surplus cell, so (rows) $\le$ (surplus), which caps the
numerator degree at $2k+1$ over the pole $(1-3z)^{k+1}$; partial fractions
then force the polynomial law with onset threshold $n\ge2k+1$ (the observed
onset, now derived), and a $3$-adic bound on the change of basis gives the
integrality. Cumulant linearity in $n$ is the residue resummation of the
same identity. The full write-up and machine checks are in the repository
(\texttt{docs/proofs/diagonal-law.md},
\texttt{experiments/diagonal\_law\_proof\_check.py}). The constants
themselves are finite local enumerations: the level-$\le5$ coefficients of
both series in the grand form (in particular
$h=25,208,1483,20688,130208$) are re-derived ab initio from the $31$
cluster types of surplus $\le5$
(\texttt{experiments/cluster\_weight\_dp.py}), and the single-row cluster
weight is exactly $(2s{+}1)^2$ for an $s$-cell row---$25=5^2$ is the $s=2$
case, and the interior weight factors as $(2s{+}1)$ per contact side.
What remains fitted rather than derived is $P_k$ for $6\le k\le18$; with
the theorem in hand those fits are interpolation of a known-shape form,
and their held-out hits remain load-bearing as checks of the
\emph{engine} rather than of the law.

\subsection{The height triangle mod 3: the spine cubic}
\label{sec:spine}

Reducing the diagonal law modulo~3 collapses the cluster gas to a single
species. In the law's units every cluster carries
$3^{\,2k-\ell-1}$ ($k$ its surplus, $\ell$ its rows), and since each cluster
row holds at least one surplus cell, $k\ge\ell$: the exponent is positive
for every type except the bare two-cell row, whose weight $25\equiv1$. The
surviving generating object is \emph{algebraic}: writing the cumulant
series as $H(t)$, one gets $H\equiv W \pmod 3$ where
\[
  W^3 \;=\; W^2 + t \qquad\text{over } \mathbb F_3 .
\]
Consequences, each machine-verified on all $420$ in-band cells of the
banked triangle: a base-3 digit-product formula
$P_k(n)\bmod 3 = [y^k]\prod_i W(y^{3^i})^{n_i}$ (with $n=\sum n_i3^i$), so
the triangle mod~3 is $3$-automatic with an explicit automaton; the spine
entries $T(3k{+}1,2k{+}1)\equiv1$ (by Lagrange inversion in characteristic
3, where $\varphi=(1-x)^3$ has vanishing derivative); and the Smith normal
form of the $N\times N$ matrix of diagonal polynomials, which has exactly
$\lceil (N-1)/3\rceil$ nontrivial invariant factors, all powers of $3$. The
finitely many congruences supporting these statements ($G\equiv1\bmod 9$
and the mod-9/mod-27 lifts of the cubic, e.g.\
$H^3\equiv H^2+25t \bmod 9$) are \emph{derived} from the same cluster
decomposition---mod~27 the master equation is a finite explicit equation in
five integers---not observed. Reading along rows: the last nonzero residue
of row $n$ cycles $1,1,2$ with $n \bmod 3$; the deficit-two case
$T(3m{+}2,2m{+}1)\equiv2$ is proved for all $m$ by a reusable device---the
family generating function is a diagonal of $G\,H^n$, formal
Lagrange--B\"urmann over $\mathbb Z/27$ collapses it to a rational
identity on the mod-27 master curve, and exact polynomial division (the
curve relation is monic) decides it. Details and verifiers:
\texttt{results/ternary-spine.md}, \texttt{results/defect-gas.md},
\texttt{experiments/ternary\_spine.py}, \texttt{experiments/defect\_gas.py},
\texttt{experiments/deficit2\_proof.py}.

\subsection{The diagonal law is lattice-universal}
\label{sec:universal}

Nothing in Theorem~\ref{thm:diaglaw} is specific to the king lattice. Call
a lattice \emph{row-local} if its cells are indexed by $\mathbb Z^2$ with
translation-invariant adjacency reaching at most one row up or down, and
let $b$ be the number of up-neighbours of a cell (the drift count). The
five steps of the proof carry over verbatim with $b$ in place of $3$:
for every such lattice, $T(H{+}k,H)=q_k(H)\,b^H$ for all $H\ge k{+}1$ with
$\deg q_k\le k$ and integer-valued $P_k=b^{1+2k}q_k$; and modulo any prime
$p\mid b$ only the pair-row survives the valuation collapse, so the mod-$p$
diagonal structure is governed by \emph{the same cubic} $H^3=H^2+wu$ over
$\mathbb F_p$, with $w$ the pair-weight residue. Machine-checked
instances: the square lattice ($b=1$: polyomino diagonals of A001168 are
plain polynomials from the same onset, density $4=2^2$); the hexagonal
lattice ($b=2$: $P_1=9n-15$ holdout-exact, density $9=3^2$, a
\emph{dyadic} spine $H^3=H^2+u$ over $\mathbb F_2$); and the king lattice
($b=3$, density $25=5^2$). The scaling $w$ is not always $1$: a
contiguous five-neighbour lattice gives $w=4\pmod 5$, and the lattice with
up-offsets $\{-2,0,2\}$ gives $w=0$ --- the degenerate branch, a lattice
whose banded triangle is trivial mod~3. Polyiamonds (period-two row
structure, outside the literal class) obey the analogous law with domino
ground states, $T(2H{-}2,H)=2^{H-2}$ and $P_1=2H$, data-grade. The
``Ternary'' Spine is thus the $p=3$ member of a lattice-indexed family;
the curve is invariant, the lattice chooses the prime and the scaling.
Proofs and checkers: \texttt{docs/proofs/universal-diagonal-law.md}.

\subsection{The diagonal-mirror triangle: quasi-polynomials, period two}
\label{sec:dmdiag}

Let $d(S,n)$ count the diagonal-mirror-fixed polyplets with $n$ cells and
bounding box exactly $S\times S$ (the box is forced square). The minimal such
animal is a bare diagonal, $n=S$, and there are exactly two ground states
(main and anti-diagonal). On the diagonal $k=n-S$ the data show, and exact
difference tables confirm over every available point:
\[
  d(S,\,S+k)\ \text{is quasi-polynomial in } S \text{ of period two and
  degree } k \text{ per parity class},
\]
with leading coefficient $S^k/k!$ and empirical onset $S\ge 2k+2$ (even) /
$2k+3$ (odd). The pinned polynomials for $k\le3$
(Table~\ref{tab:dmpk}), with $4$--$12$ holdout hits each, were subsequently
confirmed \emph{forward} on strips computed hours later on different hardware
($P_1$ hit $d(31,32)=38$; $P_2$ hit $d(30,32)=672$; $P_3$ hit
$d(29,32)=7456$); $P_4$ (even class) is pinned by two independent methods

In the generating-function basis the structure is crisper. Let $G_k$ be
the generating function of the polynomial law itself,
$G_k(x)=\sum_{S\ge0} P_k(S)\,x^S$ with $P_k$ evaluated per parity; its
coefficients agree with the counts $d(S,S+k)$ at every in-regime $S$ (the
finitely many pre-onset counts deviate from the polynomials, so the raw
counting series differs from $G_k$ by an additive polynomial of degree
below the onset, which does not affect the pole structure). Then
\[
  G_k(x)\;=\;\frac{N_k(x)}{(1-x)^{k+1}(1+x)^{k}},\qquad
  N_k \in \mathbb{Z}[x],\ \deg N_k = 2k,\qquad
  N_k(\pm1)=(\pm2)^k \ (k\ge1).
\]
The integer numerators, for the record:
\begin{align*}
  N_0 &= 2\\

The boundary condition $N_k(\pm1)=(\pm2)^k$ was at
no point imposed on any fit: the fitter solves only for polynomial
coefficients against exact diagonal counts, and the boundary values
\emph{emerged} from its output, exactly as they hold on the pinned rows
$k\le4$---where every coefficient is fixed by exact differencing with
surplus holdout points and no overfitting account is available. Its
status is an observed regularity across levels $k=1..5$, with the caveat
that at $k=5$ it is only as trustworthy as the fitted row itself.
The boundary values say the dominant single-defect family has two species per
odd footprint; the next layer of the peeled numerators follows linear-in-$k$
laws, the signature of interacting defect pairs. Exact overdetermined
elimination \emph{refutes} any low-order bivariate rational closure across
$k$ (each level carries genuinely new defect species), and the parity
structure of the $k=3$ level refutes the naive two-ground-state defect gas:
additional length-free families (anti-diagonal excursions crossing the main
spine) are present. A proof is not attempted, and we are explicit about
what one would require. The natural strategy is a decomposition argument:
show that every sufficiently large animal on this diagonal splits, in
exactly one way, into a bounded set of local pieces (stretches of clean
diagonal, the excursions above, and small clusters carrying the $k$ extra
cells), so that counting animals reduces to counting arrangements of pieces
along a line. Decompositions of that kind yield rational generating
functions; but rationality alone does not give quasi-polynomiality---for
that, every pole of $G_k$ must lie at a root of unity, and the specific law
observed here demands the poles sit at $x=\pm1$ with multiplicities exactly
$k{+}1$ and $k$. Nothing in the decomposition picture forces those
multiplicities; they are measured facts a proof must reproduce from the
count of independent free lengths available at weight $k$. This program
has now been carried out for both triangles. For the \emph{height}
triangle it is Theorem~\ref{thm:diaglaw}. For this triangle, the
decomposition is a \emph{segment grammar}: reading rows, an animal at
surplus $k$ splits into at most $2k{+}1$ perfect diagonal/anti-diagonal
segments joined at defect clusters, with at most $2k$ direction reversals
(each mirror-orbit of turns consumes cell surplus --- the reversal lemma,
tight on the data); per combinatorial type the animals are the lattice
points of a one-parameter polytope with coefficients in $\{1,2\}$, so
each type contributes a period-two quasi-polynomial, and a telescoping
rank argument caps the degree at $k$. Hence $d(S,S{+}k)$ is a period-two
quasi-polynomial of degree $\le k$ for $S$ beyond an effective onset ---
poles only at $x=\pm1$, of bounded order. The parity anomaly of the
$k=3$ level is the grammar's anti-excursion classes, not an obstruction.
What the proof does \emph{not} deliver is the sharp onset
($2k{+}2$/$2k{+}3$) and the exact multiplicity split $(k{+}1,k)$; those
remain validated data. Full write-up with the honest lemma-by-lemma
grading: \texttt{docs/proofs/dm-diagonal-law.md}.

The specific polynomials of Section~\ref{sec:dmdiag} are computed and
validated (the law's shape is proved as described above). Its
practical role is sharp: the sparse strips ($S\ge29$ at $n\ge33$) are
precisely the RAM-expensive ones---one strip burned $\sim$$62$ CPU-hours and
$79$\,GB to produce the two numbers $d(31,31)=2$, $d(31,32)=38$ that $P_0$
and $P_1$ now give for free---so the pinned polynomials replace exactly the
strips that hardware cannot reach, at the price of the \tierthree{} label.

\paragraph{The bilateral identity.}
The bilateral count equals $\tfrac12(H+D)$ exactly, by a double count. Write
the symmetry group of a fixed polyplet as a subgroup $K\le D_4$. If $K$
contains a reflection, its rotations form an index-$2$ subgroup, so exactly
half of $K$'s elements are reflections; otherwise $K$ has none. Count the
incidences $(A,m)$ of a fixed animal $A$ with a reflection
$m\in\operatorname{Stab}(A)$ in two ways. Summed over the four reflections it
is $2H+2D$, since conjugate reflections have equal fixed-counts. Summed over
free classes, a class with stabilizer order $|K|$ contains $8/|K|$ fixed
animals, each with $|K|/2$ reflections in its stabilizer when the class is
bilateral (and none otherwise)---so exactly $4$ incidences per bilateral
class. Hence $2H+2D=4\cdot(\text{bilateral classes})$. This was also checked
numerically against every published term of A030234.

\section{Hole stratification}
\label{sec:holes}

A \emph{hole} of a polyplet is a bounded connected component of its
complement. For king (8-connected) foreground the topologically consistent
choice is \textbf{4-connected} background: a single diagonal staircase of
cells then seals a region (its corner gaps are not 4-passable), matching the
Jordan-curve picture and the convention of
\href{https://oeis.org/A389193}{A389193} for ordinary polyominoes.

We stratify A006770 by hole count: let $T_{\mathrm{hole}}(n,k)$ be the number
of fixed $n$-cell polyplets with exactly $k$ holes, so
$\sum_k T_{\mathrm{hole}}(n,k)=a(n)$. The column transfer matrix carries $k$
as a second additive index via the Euler characteristic
($\#\text{holes}=1-\chi$ for a connected animal, $\chi$ accumulated by local
$2\times2$ bit-quad increments~\cite{gray}). The running $\chi$ tally is a
sum of local contributions and is \emph{interpreted} only when an animal is
complete, at which point every cavity is either enclosed by foreground (a
hole) or open to the exterior (contributing nothing to $1-\chi$); an
unfinished bay on the active frontier never enters a reported count, so no
open/closed disambiguation is required mid-sweep. The resulting counts are
exact,
byte-identical to an independent per-animal flood fill for $n\le14$, and have
row sums equal to A006770. The triangle is exact through $n=18$; rows
$15$--$18$, beyond the flood oracle, are confirmed by a second-architecture
recount (clang/ARM against GCC/x86, byte-identical), so no row rests on a
single engine. At $n=18$ there are $\num{16503616943998}$ hole-free polyplets
and $\num{4970078092354}$ with a single hole, the maximum being $10$. The
hole-free and one-hole columns are absent from OEIS (checked by Superseeker);
b-files are prepared in the release.

\paragraph{The diagonal law is hole-graded.}

\paragraph{Maximum hole area.}
How much empty area can $n$ cells enclose? Let $M(n)$ be the maximum number
of enclosed empty cells over all $n$-cell polyplets. A diamond (L$^1$-ball)
wall of $4r$ cells on $|x|+|y|=r$ is king-connected and seals the $2r^2-2r+1$
cells inside it, so $M(4r)\ge 2r^2-2r+1$ (the centered square numbers,
\href{https://oeis.org/A001844}{A001844}). This diamond is asymptotically
optimal: when the boundary is measured in the king ($L^\infty$, Chebyshev)
metric, the area-maximizing shape is the diamond, the $L^1$ ball dual to the
$L^\infty$ square (Busemann's isoperimetrix~\cite{strang}). Hence
$M(n)\sim n^2/8$, twice the polyomino rate $\sim n^2/16$, since only king
animals can run the diagonal walls the $L^1$ ball requires.

Brute force gives the exact values
$M(1{:}17)=0,0,0,1,1,2,3,5,6,8,10,13,15,18,21,25,28$, which the diamond
attains at $n=4,8,12,16$. In fact all seventeen values fit a single closed
form: they coincide with \emph{the nearest integer to $(n-2)^2/8$}---the
sequence \href{https://oeis.org/A001971}{A001971} shifted by two---which
agrees with the diamond exactly at every multiple of four, since
$\bigl[(4r-2)^2/8\bigr] = 2r^2-2r+1$.

For a single hole the nearest-integer form is proved, two-sided:

\begin{theorem}[single-hole maximum]
\label{thm:diamond}
Over $n$-cell polyplets whose enclosed empty cells form one $4$-connected
region, the maximum enclosed area is exactly
$\left\lfloor (n-2)^2/8+\tfrac12\right\rfloor$ for every $n\ge4$.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Lower bound:} parity-aligned diagonal boxes. The $4$-neighbour ring
of the diagonal box $u\in[0,a{-}1]$, $v\in[0,b{-}1]$ (diagonal coordinates
$u=x{+}y$, $v=x{-}y$) has exactly $a{+}b{+}2$ cells, is king-connected, and
encloses $\lceil ab/2\rceil$ cells; optimizing over $a{+}b=n{-}2$ attains
the bound at every residue---the diamond is the $a=b$ case, and the
``slightly asymmetric'' optima at $n\not\equiv0 \pmod 4$ are the boxes with
$|a{-}b|\in\{1,2\}$. \emph{Upper bound:} a moat-cycle argument. The hole's
contour induces a closed king-walk in the animal with winding number one
about the hole; loop-erasure extracts a simple cycle that must pass beyond
the hole's diagonal extent on all four sides, and since a king step moves
$|\Delta u|+|\Delta v|\le2$, a closed cycle spanning diagonal ranges
$h_a\times h_m$ needs at least $h_a{+}h_m{+}2$ cells. Combined with the
parity count (area $\le\lceil h_a h_m/2\rceil$) and the integer
maximization, the bound is exact at every $n$. Each step is machine-checked
end-to-end on $3{,}927$ single-hole animals; write-up and checkers in
\texttt{results/maxhole-proof.md}.

$M(17)=28$ was the first genuinely untested prediction of the formula
(fitted on $n\le16$, with no forward confirmation until this run): a
78-way split of the exact ($n{=}17$) hole-area sweep, combined by
elementwise maximum, confirms it exactly---a real forward test, not
another point it was fit to.

\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:diamond}
The same formula gives the maximum \emph{total} enclosed area over any
number of holes: splitting area across several holes is never better.
Verified exhaustively for $n\le17$ and on $6{,}000$ random multi-hole
animals with zero violations.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{openproblem}
\label{op:mn}
Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:diamond}. Filling the holes reduces it to an
isoperimetric ``peeling'' inequality for hole-free animals---the interior
of a hole-free animal has a $4$-shell at least $4$ smaller than the
animal's own ($|{\rm shell}_4(I)|\le|S|-4$), which telescopes to the
bound---verified on $7{,}645$ animals (zero violations, tight on
onion-ring shapes) but open. The first untested value of the formula
itself is $M(18)=32$ (exact, no rounding: $(18-2)^2/8=32$).
\end{openproblem}

\section{Generating functions by height, and rigorous bounds}
\label{sec:gf}

For a fixed bounding-box height $H$, the strip transfer matrix has a bounded
state space, so $T(n,H)$ satisfies a finite-order linear recurrence and its
generating function $G_H(x)=P_H(x)/Q_H(x)$ is rational~\cite{stanley}. We
recover these exactly for $H\le10$ by Berlekamp--Massey~\cite{massey} over
$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for several primes followed by CRT, each accepted
only when the fitted recurrence reproduces held-out terms. The denominators
$Q_H$ have orders\footnote{These orders are $3$-term running sums of
\emph{atom} degrees $1,2,4,9,29,68,181,\dots$: each $Q_H$ factors as
$N_{H-2}N_{H-1}N_H$, where $N_H$ is the denominator of the height-$H$ strip
generating function. The divisibility direction is algebraic, not
empirical: exact-height counts are second differences of strip counts, so
$Q_H \mid N_{H-2}N_{H-1}N_H$ by construction, for any finite-range animal
class. What is observed rather than proved is the absence of cancellation
(equality, so that every irreducible factor divides exactly three
consecutive $Q_H$), verified here for $H\le7$; a failure at larger $H$
could only \emph{shrink} $Q_H$, never introduce factors outside the three
atoms. Neither degree sequence appears in OEIS (Superseeker-checked).} $1,3,7,15,42,106,278,711,1897,5005$ for
$H=1,\dots,10$; the $H=10$ recovery, needing $\sim\!10{,}000$ series terms,
was reproduced on a second architecture byte-identically. As an example,
\[
  G_3(x)=\frac{9x^3-8x^4-2x^5+4x^6+x^7}{1-7x+15x^2-9x^3-3x^4+5x^5-x^6-x^7}.
\]

\paragraph{Hole-resolved.}
Refining by hole count keeps each fixed-$(H,k)$ row C-finite: we recover
$G_{H,k}$ for $H\le6$ (all reachable $k$), the rows $k\le2$ at $H=7$, and the
row $k=0$ at $H=8$, each validated against a fresh prime. The rationality is genuinely a fixed-height
phenomenon: the two-variable array is provably not D-finite, as we now
show.

\subsection{The by-height generating function is not D-finite}
\label{sec:notdfinite}

Let $F(x,y)=\sum_{n,H} T(n,H)\,x^n y^H$, so the fixed-height series
$G_H(x)$ are its $y$-coefficients. $F$ fails to be D-finite (it satisfies
no linear differential equation in $y$ with polynomial coefficients; since
$F$ has rational coefficients, allowing complex coefficients changes
nothing by descent to the field of definition). The proof combines four
classical facts, each stated here in the form used; write
$\mu_H$ for the growth constant of the height-$\le H$ strip counts,
so $1/\mu_H$ is the radius of convergence of $G_{\le H}$ and, by the
second-difference identity above, of $G_H$.

\emph{(i) $\mu_H$ is an algebraic integer} (Fatou's lemma~\cite{fatou}: a
rational power series with integer coefficients has a lowest-terms
representation $P/Q$ with $P,Q\in\mathbb Z[x]$ and $Q(0)=1$). The
reciprocals of the poles of $G_H$ are then roots of the monic integer
reversal of $Q_H$; the radius point $1/\mu_H$ is itself a pole, located on
the positive real axis by Pringsheim's theorem~\cite{flajolet} (the
coefficients are nonnegative), so $\mu_H$ is a root of a monic integer
polynomial.

\emph{(ii) Every Galois conjugate of $\mu_H$ has modulus at most $\mu_H$.}
The conjugates are among the reciprocal poles, and no pole has modulus
below the radius of convergence, by the definition of the radius. In the
standard terminology, the \emph{house} of the algebraic integer $\mu_H$
equals $\mu_H$ itself, and $\mu_H<\lambda\le 20000/2147$ by the rigorous
upper bound of Section~\ref{sec:result}.

\emph{(iii) The $\mu_H$ are strictly increasing, hence pairwise distinct.}
The column-signature transfer matrix of the height-$\le H$ strip
(Section~\ref{sec:methodB}) is irreducible for $x>0$: from any signature
one reaches the single-cell signature (close all components with a
spanning column), and conversely any signature is reachable from it. The
height-$\le H$ system is a proper principal submatrix of the
height-$\le(H{+}1)$ system, connected to the new states through the cycle
(single cell) $\to$ (full column) $\to$ (single cell); deleting
rows and columns of an irreducible nonnegative matrix strictly decreases
its Perron root~\cite{hornjohnson}, so the strip growth constants strictly
increase. Strictness also rules out cancellation in the second
differences, so $1/\mu_H$ is a genuine pole of $G_H$ and is not a pole of
any $G_j$ with $j<H$. (Machine check: the ten exact denominators give
$\mu_H = 1,\ 1{+}\sqrt2,\ 3.4437,\dots,5.9917$, strictly increasing.)

\emph{(iv) Only finitely many algebraic integers have degree $\le D$ and
house $\le B$} (Kronecker~\cite{kronecker}): the minimal polynomial has
integer coefficients bounded by $\binom{D}{k}B^{k}$, leaving finitely many
possibilities. With $B=20000/2147$ and the infinitely many distinct
$\mu_H$ of (iii), it follows that $\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_H\to\infty$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:notdfinite}
$F(x,y)=\sum_{n,H}T(n,H)x^ny^H$ is not D-finite.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} Suppose $\sum_{i\le r}p_i(x,y)\,\partial_y^i F=0$ with
$p_i\in\mathbb Q[x,y]$, not all zero, and let $D=\max_i\deg_x p_i$.
Extracting $y$-coefficients gives, for every $N$, a relation
$\sum_{\delta}c_\delta(x,N)\,G_{N-\delta}(x)=0$ over a finite window of
$\delta$, where each $c_\delta(x,N)$ is polynomial in $N$ of degree $\le r$
and in $x$ of degree $\le D$, and where the $c_\delta$ jointly determine
the $p_i$ (falling factorials of $N$ are linearly independent). This
extraction is the opening move of Lemma~9 of~\cite{bmr2002}; what follows
is where we depart from it. Anchor the
window so that its top face is a level $H_0$: every other face refers to a
lower height, where $G$ is finite at $x=1/\mu_{H_0}$ by (iii), while
$G_{H_0}$ has a pole there --- so the top coefficient vanishes at
$x=1/\mu_{H_0}$. A polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $\le D$ cannot
vanish at an algebraic number of degree exceeding $D$, so at every level
with $\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_{H_0}>D$ the top coefficient vanishes
identically in $x$; at $r{+}1$ such levels (which exist by (iv)) it
vanishes as a polynomial in $N$, i.e.\ the top face is identically zero.
The next face becomes the top and the same levels kill it in turn; all
faces vanish, so all $p_i=0$ --- a contradiction. $\square$

Corroboration beyond the abstract argument: the new-root contents of the
ten exact denominators have degrees $1,2,4,9,29,68,181,462,1254,3289$, and
the first eight are certified irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ (single- and
multi-prime factorization certificates), so
$\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_8=462$ already---the degree growth that (iv)
guarantees abstractly is visible concretely at rate
$\approx\sqrt\lambda$ per level. The argument uses only integer counts,
per-height rationality, and strictly increasing bounded strip constants,
so it applies verbatim to fixed polyominoes, polyhexes, and polyiamonds by
height.

\paragraph{Relation to existing work.}
Neither the conclusion nor the opening step is new. Non-D-finiteness of an
anisotropic generating function was proved for self-avoiding polygons, and
for directed bond animals, by Rechnitzer's haruspicy
programme~\cite{rechnitzer}; the criterion that programme runs on is
Lemma~9 of Bousquet-M\'elou and Rechnitzer~\cite{bmr2002}, whose proof
begins with the same coefficient extraction used above. From that shared
start the arguments diverge completely. Theirs concludes topologically ---
the union of the coefficients' pole sets has finitely many limit points,
via an asymptotic argument in the index --- and applying it demands an
explicit combinatorial description of the denominators, in the polygon
setting a product of cyclotomic polynomials. Ours concludes arithmetically
and effectively: it follows a single distinguished pole per slice, and the
only inputs are Perron--Frobenius monotonicity and a crude upper bound on
$\lambda$. That lightness is the contribution. It is why the proof
transports to any family with a column transfer matrix with no bespoke
denominator analysis, and why it yields explicit excluded $(r,D)$ boxes
rather than a bare dichotomy. Arithmetic constraints on D-finite series do
exist: the \emph{D-finiteness, rationality and height} theorems of Bell,
Nguyen and Zannier~\cite{bnz2020} and their multivariate
P\'olya--Carlson successor, and the height-gap results of Bell, Hu and
Satriano~\cite{bhs2020}. Both bound the Weil heights of the
\emph{coefficients} of a series, or of a sequence read along the orbit of a
rational map, and conclude rationality; neither speaks about the growth
constants of a family of slices, and we found no application of either line
to a lattice model. We have not located the use of Northcott
finiteness on a family of slice growth constants elsewhere; a literature
search cannot establish absence, so we claim the argument as ours with
that qualification and nothing stronger.

One limitation deserves recording. Klazar~\cite{klazar} proves
non-holonomicity for a class of divergent series after enlarging the target
from D-finite to $D_A$-finite --- analytic rather than polynomial
coefficients --- on the ground that polynomiality was never carrying the
argument. Ours admits no such enlargement: the step ``a polynomial over
$\mathbb Q$ of degree $\le D$ cannot vanish at an algebraic number of degree
exceeding $D$'' \emph{is} the dichotomy, and an analytic coefficient may
vanish at $1/\mu_{H_0}$ without vanishing identically.
Theorem~\ref{thm:notdfinite} is therefore a statement about D-finiteness
over $\mathbb Q(x)$ and does not reach $D_A$-finiteness. The isotropic
$a(x)$ remains open, exactly as in the polygon setting.

\paragraph{Rigorous lower bounds.}
Summing the fixed-height GFs gives $a(n)\ge\sum_{H\le 10}T(n,H)$ exactly for
any $n$, by extracting coefficients from rational functions. When this bound
was computed, $a(25)$ was out of reach and the bound gave
$a(25)\ge\num{6657105903966723073}$; the exact value
$\num{14994811325186658577}$, computed later by Method~C, confirms it---a
retrospective certification of the GF machinery. The bound remains
available for any $n$, though its capture fraction shrinks as taller
strips dominate: $44\%$ of the exact value at $n=25$, $16\%$ at $n=34$.
(When first computed the bound gave $a(35)\ge5.07\times10^{26}$,
comfortably under the value $\num{3561147281381782175236253062}$ later
computed by Method~C; likewise its
$a(40)\ge\num{4266005101622209395058618248135}$, an exact integer from
rational arithmetic, stated here in an earlier draft while $a(40)$ was
still out of reach, is confirmed by the now-exact value---the bound
captures $7.5\%$ of it. Both are retrospective certifications of the
machinery.) Beyond the final computed term it still gives exact bounds
for any $n$ (\texttt{paper/gf\_bound.py}).

\section{Reach and limits}
\label{sec:reach}

Method C's cost structure is set by its tallest surviving sweep height: at
fixed $H$ the strip's state space is bounded and cost grows only about
linearly in $n$, while adding a unit of height multiplies it by roughly the
per-height frontier growth. As each new diagonal was certified the
tallest real sweep advanced slowly---$H=18$ through $a(34)$, $H=19$ for
$a(35)$--$a(37)$, $H=20$ for $a(38)$--$a(39)$, and $H=21$ for
$a(40)$---while the cost per term grew steeply along the same ladder
(Appendix~\ref{sec:profile}): the binding resource became disk rather
than time (the $a(40)$ run's external shuffle peaked at $363$\,GB), and
the final terms ran their tall poles as solo phases at reduced core
counts to fit the disk budget. The deeper constraint is verification, not
compute---the frontier is always one term ahead of its own closed-form
certification, which is what the \tiertwominus{} grade on the final two
terms records (Section~\ref{sec:confirm}); the sequence closes at
$a(40)$ with that grade stated rather than outrun. The symmetric counts scale as
$\sqrt{a}$-class problems and reached $n=34$ for three of the four types;
the diagonal-mirror type, whose forced-square bounding box compresses least,
is the binding constraint on the remaining companions---its per-strip RAM
peaked at $83$\,GB at $n=32$ after a compact-frontier rewrite (flat sharded
arrays, ranged count vectors, $\approx1.6\times$ RAM and $\approx2\times$
speed over the hash-map frontier it replaced), and the pinned
quasi-polynomials of Section~\ref{sec:dmdiag} substitute for the strips
beyond hardware at $n\ge33$ under the \tierthree{} label.
