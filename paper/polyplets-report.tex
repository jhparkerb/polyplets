\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A \emph{polyplet} (king-move animal) is a finite set of unit cells of the square
lattice connected through shared edges or corners. OEIS sequence
\href{https://oeis.org/A006770}{A006770} counts the \emph{fixed} polyplets of $n$
cells, previously known through $n=18$. We report twenty-two new terms, through
\[
  a(40) = \num{56749893611764175164545926946127},
\]
each computed from scratch and reproducing every prior term. Every number
carries an explicit confidence tier: $a(1)$--$a(22)$ are confirmed by two
algorithms sharing no counting logic (direct enumeration and the transfer
matrix); $a(23)$--$a(38)$ by a single algorithm family, cross-checked per
term against \emph{held-out} closed forms for the top diagonals of the
height triangle; $a(39)$ and $a(40)$ lack that held-out check on their top
swept stratum, and since the sequence ends here it never will. The tiers
record that. The one-sided companion A030233 is extended to
$n=34$, and the free, bilateral, and asymmetric companions (A030222,
A030234, A030235, A194596) to $n=32$.

The machinery also yields structural results. The diagonals of the height
triangle obey an exponential/cumulant law whose shape---a polynomial of
degree $k$ times $3^{\,n-1-3k}$, with its onset and integrality---is
\emph{proved} here by an exact walk-plus-clusters decomposition, and is
lattice-universal, the mod-$p$ ``spine cubic'' $W^3=W^2+t$ having its prime
set by the lattice's drift count.
The by-height generating function $\sum T(n,H)x^ny^H$ is \emph{not
D-finite}, by a Northcott-style argument on the strip growth constants that
is lighter than the existing haruspicy route and transports to polyominoes,
polyhexes, and polyiamonds. We bracket the growth constant rigorously,
$6.543\le\lambda\le9.3153$, both ends by machine-checkable exact
certificates, the upper bound apparently the first recorded for this
lattice (the numerical estimate is $\lambda\approx7.111$). Finally, A006770
is stratified by hole count through $n=18$. Results computed and validated but not
proved are labeled as such; all code, data, and a claim-verifier are
released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The enumeration of lattice animals (finite connected sets of cells on a
regular lattice) is a classical problem at the meeting point of combinatorics and
statistical physics~\cite{klarner,jensen2001}. On the square lattice the two
standard families are \emph{polyominoes}, connected through shared edges, and
\emph{polyplets}, connected through shared edges or corners. The fixed counts
grow exponentially, so exact values are known only for modest $n$ and each new
term is a small computational milestone. The fixed-polyomino sequence
(\href{https://oeis.org/A001168}{A001168}) has been pushed
furthest~\cite{redelmeier,jensen2003,barequet}; the fixed-polyplet sequence
A006770 had been recorded through $n=18$.

Animals are counted \textbf{fixed} (up to translation; rotations and
reflections distinct), \textbf{one-sided} (up to translation and rotation),
or \textbf{free} (up to all isometries); A006770 is the fixed count. The
\emph{height triangle} $T(n,H)$ refines it, counting the $n$-cell animals of
bounding-box height exactly $H$, so $a(n)=\sum_H T(n,H)$.

This document is a \emph{computational report}: its claims are numbers, the
provenance and verification of those numbers, and the structure found in
them. It reports $a(19)$ through $a(40)$ and the results that grew out of
computing them. It is an amateur contribution carried out in collaboration
with an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude Code); the author set the goals,
made the design and verification decisions, ran the machines, and is
responsible for correctness. The data appears in the document as well as
the repository, so that the next person to attempt these computations can
use it.

\paragraph{Growth.}
The successive ratios $r_n=a(n)/a(n{-}1)$ climb slowly:
$r_{20}\approx6.765$, $r_{27}\approx6.853$, $r_{34}\approx6.905$,
$r_{40}\approx6.935$. Least-squares fits of the two-parameter ansatz
$r_n=\lambda(1+\theta/n)$ over trailing windows starting at $n=10$, $15$,
$20$, $25$ give $\lambda=7.102$, $7.104$, $7.106$, $7.107$ ($\theta$
between $-0.95$ and $-0.96$); the upward drift with window start is the
signature of an unmodelled confluent correction. Adding a confluent term
$c/n^{1+\Delta_1}$ and scanning $\Delta_1$, the exponent
$\Delta_1=\tfrac12$ gives the smallest residuals on every window (by
factors of ${\sim}4$ to ${\sim}140$) and removes the drift:
$\lambda=7.1108$--$7.1111$ across the same four windows, with $\theta$
between $-1.02$ and $-1.03$, consistent with the universal
two-dimensional lattice-animal value $\theta=-1$~\cite{jensen2001}, which
the fit was free to miss. A first-order differential-approximant analysis
(calibrated on synthetic $n^\theta\lambda^n$ data and stable across
rescalings and the number of trailing terms used) independently locates
the dominant singularity at $\lambda=7.110$ with exponent
$\theta=-1.000$. We accordingly estimate $\lambda\approx7.111$. This is an
estimate, not a rigorous bound; the two-sided bracket follows.

\paragraph{A rigorous lower bound on $\lambda$.}
Polyplets of bounding-box height at most $H$ are a subfamily of all
polyplets, so their growth constant $\mu_H$ (the dominant eigenvalue of the
height-$\le H$ strip transfer operator $M(x)$, whose entries are powers of
$x$ counting cells placed) satisfies $\mu_H\le\lambda$ for every $H$, and
$\mu_H$ increases with $H$. Each rung is certified rather than measured. Since
$\rho(M(x))$ is increasing in $x$ and equals $1$ at $x=1/\mu_H$, exhibiting a
nonnegative integer vector $v\ne0$ and a rational $x$ with $M(x)v\ge v$
componentwise proves $\rho(M(x))\ge1$, hence $\mu_H\ge1/x$; the lower half of
the Collatz--Wielandt formula needs neither positivity of $v$ nor
irreducibility of $M$~\cite{hornjohnson}. The witness is a finite object and
the check is exact integer arithmetic, so a reader re-verifies the bound
without rerunning the search that found it. Carrying the ladder to $H=17$
($6{,}536{,}381$ states) gives
\[
  \lambda \;\ge\; \mu_{17} \;\ge\; 6.543 ,
\]
with certificates at every rung from the independent anchor
$\mu_2=1+\sqrt2$ upward. The ladder converges to $\lambda$ from below, but
slowly: the rungs gain about $0.05$ each while cost grows ${\sim}3.5\times$
per rung, reaching $11.8$\,GB at $H=17$. It is stopped here rather than
exhausted. It supersedes the closed-form bound available from a solvable
subclass, Bacher's directed king animals~\cite{bacher}, whose dominant
singularity gives $\lambda\ge3+2\sqrt2\approx5.828$ with no computation at
all ($\lambda\gtrsim6.475$ numerically for his multi-directed subclass).

\paragraph{A rigorous upper bound on $\lambda$.}
No upper bound on $\lambda$ appears in the literature. We obtain one by a
Bui-style finite-type convolution certificate~\cite{bui2025}: mark a cell of the
animal, case on the occupancy of a single neighbour at a time, and record the
resulting local patterns as a finite set of ``types'' whose generating functions
satisfy a system of polynomial convolution inequalities
$\varphi_T \le \varphi_{T'} + \varphi_{T'}\varphi_D$. A positive rational vector
$u$ with $u_T \ge F_x(u)_T$ for every type, checked in exact rational
arithmetic, proves the system bounded at $x$, hence $\lambda \le 1/x$. With a
split window of radius $3$ (a $5930$-type system) the certificate $x=2147/20000$
gives
\[
  \lambda \;\le\; \tfrac{20000}{2147} \;\approx\; 9.3153 ,
\]
verified with no floating point in the deductive chain. Together with the
certified strip ladder this brackets the growth constant two-sided,
$6.543 \le \lambda \le 9.3153$, both ends machine-checkable in exact
arithmetic and consistent with the estimate $\lambda\approx7.111$. The gap
reflects a structural limit: the convolution over-count grows with animal
size, so finite-type methods of this class floor strictly above $\lambda$.

\section{Methods: an escalation ladder}
\label{sec:methods}

Four counting methods (A--D) computed these numbers, with closed forms
(Section~\ref{sec:heightdiag}) replacing the top diagonals, a sweep's most
expensive and least populated cells; each subsection states what ran out and
what replaced it. All engines share three disciplines: exact integer
arithmetic; every production run re-derives \emph{all} smaller terms, so a
regression anywhere in range is visible; and admissible pruning only (a prune
may only ever \emph{lose} animals, so reproducing the published range
certifies that it lost none).

\subsection{Method A: direct enumeration (to $n=22$)}
\label{sec:methodA}

Redelmeier's backtracking enumeration~\cite{redelmeier}, adapted to king
adjacency, grows every animal exactly once from an anchored cell: it maintains
an ordered frontier of \emph{untried} neighbours and recurses, with the
standard rule that a cell once placed or rejected at a level is excluded from
deeper levels. The recursion tallies by size; no animal is stored. Being
embarrassingly parallel by sub-tree, the count was run as two decorrelated
campaigns, on Apple silicon (Apple~clang, frontier split $7\times64$) and
AMD/x86 (GCC, split $8\times96$). The per-size outputs are
\textbf{byte-identical}. Differing in hardware, compiler, and scheduling,
the two campaigns make a transient or platform-specific fault implausible;
what they cannot jointly catch is a bug in the shared \emph{algorithm},
which the transfer matrix (Methods~B,~C) rules out by
counting the same animals a completely different way. An optimized kernel (the terminal frontier level counted in
bulk, compile-time neighbour offsets; ${\approx}2\times$ faster) run as a
three-architecture fleet (ARM, x86, Apple silicon) extended the whole-row
enumeration to $n=22$, byte-identical across machines and matching the
transfer-matrix totals term for term. Direct enumeration and the transfer
matrix are thus two independent algorithms agreeing through $a(22)$, which
is the \tierone{} anchor.

\subsection{Method B: the column transfer matrix (to $n\approx23$)}
\label{sec:methodB}

The second method counts the same animals by an entirely different principle:
dynamic programming over the connectivity of a moving boundary, with no animal
materialised, a king adaptation of the transfer-matrix / finite-lattice
technique for polyominoes~\cite{jensen2001,jensen2003,bousquet}. Animals of
bounding-box height exactly $H$ are counted separately, so
$a(n)=\sum_{H=1}^{n}T(n,H)$; the heights are independent, checkpointable
sub-problems.

Within a height, confine the animal to an $H$-row strip and sweep columns left
to right. The \emph{boundary signature} records the occupancy of the most
recent column, a partition of its filled cells into the partial animal's
connected components, and two flags for whether rows $0$ and $H-1$ have yet
been touched. It is a Markov state, since a future cell attaches only to the
current column or later. Because king adjacency reaches the diagonal neighbours
$(c{-}1,r{\pm}1)$, the transfer moves an \emph{entire column} at once, keeping
the previous column wholly present; connectivity across the step is resolved
by union--find. Three invariants make accepted sweeps biject with fixed
polyplets: the first column is column $0$ (no horizontal double-counting); an
animal is tallied only with both touch-flags set (height exactly $H$); and
only when its boundary is a single component. Partial states that can never
reconnect are pruned. A size-budget prune drops any state whose cells-so-far
plus an \emph{admissible} lower bound on cells-still-needed exceeds $n$.

Concretely, a state is a fixed-width byte string: one byte per strip row
holding the cell's connected-component label ($0$ for empty), with labels
\emph{canonically renumbered in first-occurrence order} after every
transition (the normalization that prevents equivalent partitions from
proliferating as distinct states), plus the two touch flags; the value
attached to a state is its vector of counts by size (layout in
\texttt{core/signature.h}).

Method B confirmed $a(19)$ against Method A (byte-identical totals) and
carried the sequence into the early twenties before its state space, which
keeps a whole column of connectivity alive per step, outgrew memory.

\subsection{Method C: the cell-at-a-time transfer matrix (to $n=40$)}
\label{sec:methodC}

The production engine for $a(24)$--$a(40)$ replaces the column step with a
\emph{cell-at-a-time} step: the boundary advances one cell per transition,
so the frontier is a staircase---$r$ cells of the new column above $H-r$
cells of the old---plus one retained north-west \emph{carry} cell, kept
because king adjacency lets the next cell reach diagonally back across the
kink in the staircase; without it the step would sever connectivity
information the lattice still provides. The state encoding is as in
Method~B (canonical component-label bytes over the staircase cells and the
carry, touch flags, counts), and each transition decides a single cell,
resolving connectivity by union--find over its at-most-three placed
neighbours. The finer step multiplies the number of transitions but shrinks
the live state space and, decisively, the volume that must be reshuffled
between passes (measured speedups over Method B grow from $\sim$29$\times$
at $H=12$ to $\sim$200$\times$ at $H=16$). At frontier sizes beyond RAM the
state map spills to disk: the frontier is hash-sharded, expanded shard by
shard, and merged by external passes, so the binding resource is disk
bandwidth rather than memory. Heights remain independent jobs; per-height
results are banked with their provenance and re-summed by a combiner that
refuses partial or duplicated inputs.

\paragraph{I/O integrity.}
A multi-hour external shuffle can fail in mid-flight, so the disk
layer fails closed rather than losing states silently: spill files carry a
record count, so truncation (including the short writes produced by
resource exhaustion) is detected on read rather than absorbed; readers and
the merge refuse malformed or count-mismatched inputs; and the per-height
combiner refuses missing, empty, or duplicated shards. These refusal paths
are not assumed to work: the gate suite fault-injects each mode and
requires the pipeline to abort loudly.

Each production run reproduces the complete triangle (every $T(n,H)$ for all
smaller $n$), so each new term arrives with a full-chain regression against
everything already known, including the \tierone{} range.

\paragraph{Which cells are computed and which are injected.}
The headline totals contain both, so the split must be stated exactly. For
$a(40)$, heights $3\le H\le21$ were swept for real ($95.9\%$ of the total);
$H=1,2$ are elementary closed forms; and $H\ge22$, the diagonals
$k\le18$, were \emph{injected} from the fitted closed forms of
Section~\ref{sec:heightdiag}, contributing $4.1\%$ of $a(40)$. The
analogous split holds for the preceding terms, the injection boundary
advancing as each new $P_k$ is certified (at $a(36)$: real sweep
$H\!=\!3$--$19$, injection $5.6\%$); the range $n\le19$ contains no
injected cells at all. Method C's cost is set by its tallest surviving
sweep height, which advanced only as each new diagonal was certified:
$H=18$ through $a(34)$, $H=19$ for $a(35)$--$a(37)$, $H=20$ for
$a(38)$--$a(39)$, $H=21$ for $a(40)$. The discipline governing injection is strict and
uniform: a $P_k$ is fitted on real-swept cells of earlier terms, must
reproduce every other real-swept cell on its diagonal (holdout), must then
predict the \emph{next} term's real-swept cell on that diagonal
exactly (the newest diagonal is always swept for real once more before its
formula is trusted), and only after surviving that live prediction is it
injected for subsequent terms. Section~\ref{sec:confirm} gives the
resulting holdout chain, and the one formula that never earned injection.
Readers who want totals free of any formula assistance may sum
Table~\ref{tab:byheight40}'s rows $H\le21$ against the banked per-height
data.

\subsection{Method D: symmetric transfer matrices (the companions)}
\label{sec:methodD}

The companion counts need, for each symmetry $g$ of the square, the number of
fixed animals invariant under $g$. An animal fixed by $g$ is determined by a
fundamental-domain slice of itself, so these counts behave like
$\sqrt{a(n)}$-scale problems and can reach $n=34$ where the full count cannot
be re-derived independently. Two engines compute them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item An \emph{orbit-graph Redelmeier} enumerator (explicit, per-animal):
        growth over cell-orbits under $\langle g\rangle$. Being explicit it is
        the cross-algorithm oracle, and it carried all four symmetry types to
        $n=24$.
  \item A family of \emph{symmetric transfer matrices}, one per conjugacy
        class: a palindromic-column sweep for the axis mirror; a half-plane
        sweep with a self-gluing seam for the $180^\circ$ rotation (with a
        transpose restriction, counting each animal from its wide
        orientation, that collapses the tall strips); and, for the diagonal
        mirror, an exact-bounding-box \emph{hook} sweep in which the frontier is the
        L-shaped shell of a growing square, swept corner outward, with the
        mirror symmetry enforced by construction. The $90^\circ$ count, whose
        invariant animals have $\sim n/4$ free cells, stays within the
        explicit enumerator's reach through $n=34$.
\end{itemize}

The two engines agree (live, in the release's test gate) for every type at
small $n$, and byte-identical at the $n=24$ oracle; beyond $n=24$ the
transfer-matrix values are single-algorithm (\tiertwo{}), validated by prefix
against the oracle range on every run. The diagonal-mirror sweep is the
expensive one: its bounding box is forced square, so the frontier
compresses least. It is farmed across machines as disjoint
exact-bounding-box strips whose partial totals sum exactly by construction;
the completed $n=32$ farm prefix-matches both the $n\le24$ oracle range and
the earlier $n\le28$ run exactly.

\section{Validation architecture}
\label{sec:confirm}

The verification burden shifts across the sequence, and the checks are
designed around the weakest link at each stage.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{External truth.} Every method reproduces A006770 exactly for
        every $n\le18$, on every production run (the triangle re-derives all
        smaller terms as a by-product).
  \item \textbf{Direct enumeration $=$ transfer matrix through $a(22)$.}
        Byte-identical totals from algorithmically disjoint counters: Method~A
        against Method~B at $a(19)$, and, after the kernel speedup and the
        three-architecture fleet run, direct whole-row enumeration against
        Method~C's totals through $a(22)$. This is the \tierone{} anchor.
  \item \textbf{Cross-architecture recounts.} $a(34)$'s full triangle was
        recomputed on a second architecture and compiler (x86/GCC against
        ARM/clang production) and is byte-identical, row by row; earlier
        terms had per-term recounts of the heaviest strata. Beyond $a(34)$
        the recount coverage weakens: $a(35)$'s second-machine
        pass covered only part of the height range; $a(36)$ was computed
        with its heights \emph{split} across two architectures (x86 for
        $H\le18$ and $H\ge20$, ARM for the $H=19$ long pole) rather than
        recomputed whole; and $a(37)$--$a(40)$ were computed on a single
        machine (ARM) with no second-architecture pass at all. Their
        decorrelation rests on the full-chain reproduction and the held-out
        diagonal checks below. Two mitigations at the final term: the
        $H=20$ column of $a(40)$ was re-swept standalone from a fresh
        checkpoint and is byte-identical, and the $H=21$ sweep carries the
        nineteen-row closed-form cross-check described next.
  \item \textbf{Held-out diagonal closed forms.} Each $P_k$ was fitted on
        the smallest admissible terms and then required to reproduce every
        later term's cell $T(n,n-k)$ exactly. The chain runs the full
        ladder: $P_{15}\to T(33,18)$, $P_{16}\to T(35,19)$,
        $P_{17}\to T(37,20)$, $P_{18}\to T(39,21)$, each formula pinned
        before the term it went on to predict existed. A passed holdout
        retroactively certifies the top cells its formula was fitted on (a
        degree-$k$ polynomial through a wrong fit point does not predict
        the next term's cell exactly), which is what holds
        $a(33)$--$a(38)$ at \tiertwo{}. The strongest single check came
        with the final term: the $a(40)$ run's real $H=21$ sweep
        reproduced the closed-form values $P_k(n)\,3^{n-1-3k}$ on every one
        of its rows $n=21$--$39$ simultaneously, re-confirming all nineteen
        wired levels at a fresh point each, in the very sweep that produced
        $T(40,21)$. The \tiertwominus{} grade attaches to $a(39)$ and
        $a(40)$: their top real cells $T(39,20)$ and $T(40,21)$ lie on
        diagonal $k=19$, and $P_{19}$, fitted from exactly these two
        cells, has no independent holdout, nor ever will, the sequence
        closing at $a(40)$. Those strata run the same code path the lower
        $P_k$ certified. It is the top cells that lack certification, not
        the code path.
  \item \textbf{Modular consistency (a design capability, not a production
        gate).} A residue-counter/CRT-reconstruction path exists and is
        regression-tested (independent residue systems must agree, which
        would catch overflow and transient corruption, though not the
        shared enumeration bugs the items above target), but it has only
        been exercised at small $n$, never against a production frontier
        sweep $a(20)$--$a(40)$. Overflow protection for the reported terms
        comes instead from the recounts above, a u128-vs-u64 comparison at
        the sizes where both are feasible, and the structural and
        fault-injection checks below.
  \item \textbf{Structural cross-checks.} The transfer matrices'
        (height,\,size) and (size,\,perimeter) marginals match the explicit
        enumeration's; rook- and bishop-connected sub-counts reproduce
        A001168 (a purely diagonal polyplet lives on one checkerboard colour
        class, itself a square lattice, so it is a polyomino in disguise);
        the $H$-cell height-$H$ diagonal is exactly $3^{H-1}$;
        Burnside numerators satisfy their $/4$ and $/8$ integrality at every
        term.
  \item \textbf{Fault injection.} Planted errors are caught (a dropped
        diagonal at $n=2$, a doubled count at $n=1$, a prune off-by-one at
        $n=10$) while a provably count-preserving change survives: the
        checks fail on wrong programs and pass on right ones.
  \item \textbf{Hygiene.} Every engine is AddressSanitizer/UBSan clean at
        gate scale, the sanitizer build compiling the same source the
        $-O3$ production binary does; the multithreaded paths are
        bit-identical to serial; every kept result traces to a clean-tree
        commit stamp in a provenance ledger; and the cross-checks are
        encoded as gates that run before release.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Availability.} The repository holds the source and the runnable
gates, the per-height triangle rows for every term through $n=40$ with their
provenance ledgers, the b-files for A006770 and its companions, and a
self-contained checker that re-verifies this paper's numerical claims.

\section{The diagonal structure of king-animal triangles}
\label{sec:diagonals}

Both triangles in this paper (the height triangle of the full count and the
exact-bounding-box triangle of the diagonal-mirror count) turn out to have
exactly solvable top diagonals. We record the laws, the fitting discipline,
and what is proved versus pinned.

\subsection{The height triangle: an exponential/cumulant law}
\label{sec:heightdiag}

A height-$H$ animal of exactly $H$ cells is one cell per row drifting by
$\{-1,0,+1\}$: $T(H,H)=3^{H-1}$. On the diagonal $k=n-H$ ($k$ cells above
minimum), the normalized counts
$P_k(n) = T(n,\,n-k)\,\cdot 3^{\,1+3k-n}$
satisfy
\[
  P_k(n) \;=\; [y^k]\,\exp\!\Big(\sum_{j\ge1}(a_j+b_j\,n)\,y^j\Big),
\]
an exponential of \emph{cumulants linear in $n$}: $b_j$ is the per-site
density of connected weight-$j$ defect clusters against the random-walk
background, $a_j$ the boundary correction, so each level costs exactly two
new constants, fitted from two data points and validated on all others. The
leading coefficient is $25^k/k!$ (twenty-five single-defect species per walk
site, an integer the fit must and does reproduce at every level), and $P_k$
times $k!$ must be integral. Levels $k\le18$ are fitted, validated, and wired
into production (Section~\ref{sec:methodC}); each was pinned on the earliest
admissible terms and holds on every later one. (A nineteenth level, $P_{19}$,
is determined by the final two top cells and passes the leading-coefficient
and integrality checks, but with the sequence closing at $a(40)$ it has no
holdout and is never used; we record it without certifying it.)

The law's shape is a theorem:

\begin{theorem}[diagonal law]
\label{thm:diaglaw}
For every $k\ge0$ there is a polynomial $q_k$ of degree $\le k$ with
$T(H{+}k,\,H)=q_k(H)\,3^H$ for \emph{all} $H\ge k+1$---equivalently
$T(n,n{-}k)=P_k(n)\,3^{\,n-1-3k}$ for all $n\ge 2k+1$ with
$P_k(n)=3^{1+2k}q_k(n{-}k)$---and $P_k$ takes integer values at every
integer.
\end{theorem}

The proof is an exact decomposition. Reading an animal row by row, a
single-cell row is a cut vertex (a king step changes the row index by at
most one), so every animal splits uniquely into a drift walk decorated by
finitely many \emph{cluster} types (maximal runs of multi-cell rows), each
with a finite configuration count; this gives an exact rational identity
for $\sum_{n,H}T(n,H)\,y^{\,n-H}z^H$, verified cell-for-cell against the
computed triangle including all boundary corrections. Every cluster row
carries at least one surplus cell, so (rows) $\le$ (surplus), which caps the
numerator degree at $2k+1$ over the pole $(1-3z)^{k+1}$; partial fractions
then force the polynomial law with onset threshold $n\ge2k+1$ (the observed
onset, now derived), and a $3$-adic bound on the change of basis gives the
integrality. Cumulant linearity in $n$ is the residue resummation of the
same identity. The constants themselves are finite local enumerations: the
level-$\le5$ coefficients are re-derived ab initio from the $31$ cluster
types of surplus $\le5$, and the single-row cluster weight is exactly
$(2s{+}1)^2$ for an $s$-cell row, of which $25=5^2$ is the $s=2$ case. What
remains fitted rather than derived is $P_k$ for $6\le k\le18$; with the
theorem in hand those fits are interpolation of a known-shape form, and
their held-out hits stay load-bearing as checks of the \emph{engine} rather
than of the law. Write-up and machine checks:
\texttt{docs/proofs/diagonal-law.md}.

\subsection{The triangle mod 3}
\label{sec:spine}

Reducing the diagonal law modulo~3 collapses the cluster gas to a single
species: every cluster carries $3^{\,2k-\ell-1}$ ($k$ its surplus, $\ell$
its rows) with $k\ge\ell$, so the exponent is positive for every type
except the bare two-cell row, whose weight $25\equiv1$. What survives is
\emph{algebraic}: writing the cumulant series as $H(t)$, one gets
$H\equiv W\pmod 3$ where
\[
  W^3 \;=\; W^2 + t \qquad\text{over } \mathbb F_3 ,
\]
whence a base-3 digit-product formula
$P_k(n)\bmod 3 = [y^k]\prod_i W(y^{3^i})^{n_i}$ (with $n=\sum n_i3^i$): the
triangle mod~3 is $3$-automatic, with an explicit automaton. The
congruences this rests on are \emph{derived} from the same cluster
decomposition rather than observed, and every consequence is
machine-verified on all $420$ in-band cells of the banked triangle
(\texttt{results/ternary-spine.md}). The law is not king-specific: for any
lattice whose adjacency reaches one row with $b$ up-neighbours the same
proof gives $T(H{+}k,H)=q_k(H)b^H$, and mod any $p\mid b$ the diagonals are
governed by the cubic $W^3=W^2+t$
(\texttt{docs/proofs/universal-diagonal-law.md}).

\paragraph{The bilateral identity.}
The bilateral count equals $\tfrac12(H+D)$ exactly, by a double count of the
incidences $(A,m)$ of a fixed animal $A$ with a reflection
$m\in\operatorname{Stab}(A)$. Summed over the four reflections this is
$2H+2D$, since conjugate reflections have equal fixed-counts. Summed over
free classes it is exactly $4$ per bilateral class: a stabilizer $K\le D_4$
containing a reflection has its rotations as an index-$2$ subgroup, so half
its elements are reflections, and a class with stabilizer order $|K|$
contains $8/|K|$ fixed animals each contributing $|K|/2$. Hence
$2H+2D=4\cdot(\text{bilateral classes})$, also checked numerically against
every published term of A030234.

\section{Hole stratification}
\label{sec:holes}

A \emph{hole} of a polyplet is a bounded connected component of its
complement. For king (8-connected) foreground the topologically consistent
choice is \textbf{4-connected} background: a single diagonal staircase of
cells then seals a region (its corner gaps are not 4-passable), matching the
Jordan-curve picture and the convention of
\href{https://oeis.org/A389193}{A389193} for ordinary polyominoes.

We stratify A006770 by hole count: let $T_{\mathrm{hole}}(n,k)$ be the number
of fixed $n$-cell polyplets with exactly $k$ holes, so
$\sum_k T_{\mathrm{hole}}(n,k)=a(n)$. The column transfer matrix carries $k$
as a second additive index via the Euler characteristic
($\#\text{holes}=1-\chi$ for a connected animal, $\chi$ accumulated by local
$2\times2$ bit-quad increments~\cite{gray}). The running $\chi$ tally is a
sum of local contributions and is \emph{interpreted} only when an animal is
complete, at which point every cavity is either enclosed by foreground (a
hole) or open to the exterior (contributing nothing to $1-\chi$); an
unfinished bay on the active frontier never enters a reported count, so no
open/closed disambiguation is required mid-sweep. The resulting counts are
exact,
byte-identical to an independent per-animal flood fill for $n\le14$, and have
row sums equal to A006770. The triangle is exact through $n=18$; rows

\section{Generating functions by height, and rigorous bounds}
\label{sec:gf}

For a fixed bounding-box height $H$, the strip transfer matrix has a bounded
state space, so $T(n,H)$ satisfies a finite-order linear recurrence and its
generating function $G_H(x)=P_H(x)/Q_H(x)$ is rational~\cite{stanley}. We
recover these exactly for $H\le10$ by Berlekamp--Massey~\cite{massey} over
$\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for several primes followed by CRT, each accepted
only when the fitted recurrence reproduces held-out terms. The denominators
$Q_H$ have orders\footnote{Each $Q_H$ factors as $N_{H-2}N_{H-1}N_H$, where
$N_H$ is the denominator of the height-$H$ strip generating function, so
these orders are $3$-term running sums of the \emph{atom} degrees
$1,2,4,9,29,68,181,\dots$. The divisibility is algebraic, not empirical:
exact-height counts are second differences of strip counts, so
$Q_H \mid N_{H-2}N_{H-1}N_H$ by construction, for any finite-range animal
class. Observed rather than proved is the absence of cancellation, verified
here for $H\le7$; a failure at larger $H$ could only \emph{shrink} $Q_H$,
never introduce factors outside the three atoms. Neither degree sequence
appears in OEIS (Superseeker-checked).} $1,3,7,15,42,106,278,711,1897,5005$ for
$H=1,\dots,10$; the $H=10$ recovery, needing $\sim\!10{,}000$ series terms,
was reproduced on a second architecture byte-identically. As an example,
\[
  G_3(x)=\frac{9x^3-8x^4-2x^5+4x^6+x^7}{1-7x+15x^2-9x^3-3x^4+5x^5-x^6-x^7}.
\]

Refining by hole count keeps each fixed-$(H,k)$ row C-finite, and those
generating functions are recovered too, through $H\le6$. The rationality is
genuinely a fixed-height phenomenon: the two-variable array is provably not
D-finite, as we now show.

\subsection{The by-height generating function is not D-finite}
\label{sec:notdfinite}

Let $F(x,y)=\sum_{n,H} T(n,H)\,x^n y^H$, so the fixed-height series
$G_H(x)$ are its $y$-coefficients. $F$ fails to be D-finite (it satisfies
no linear differential equation in $y$ with polynomial coefficients; since
$F$ has rational coefficients, allowing complex coefficients changes
nothing by descent to the field of definition). The proof combines four
classical facts, each stated here in the form used; write
$\mu_H$ for the growth constant of the height-$\le H$ strip counts,
so $1/\mu_H$ is the radius of convergence of $G_{\le H}$ and, by the
second-difference identity above, of $G_H$.

\emph{(i) $\mu_H$ is an algebraic integer} (Fatou's lemma~\cite{fatou}: a
rational power series with integer coefficients has a lowest-terms
representation $P/Q$ with $P,Q\in\mathbb Z[x]$ and $Q(0)=1$). The
reciprocals of the poles of $G_H$ are then roots of the monic integer
reversal of $Q_H$; the radius point $1/\mu_H$ is itself a pole, located on
the positive real axis by Pringsheim's theorem~\cite{flajolet} (the
coefficients are nonnegative), so $\mu_H$ is a root of a monic integer
polynomial.

\emph{(ii) Every Galois conjugate of $\mu_H$ has modulus at most $\mu_H$.}
The conjugates are among the reciprocal poles, and no pole has modulus
below the radius of convergence, by the definition of the radius. In the
standard terminology, the \emph{house} of the algebraic integer $\mu_H$
equals $\mu_H$ itself, and $\mu_H<\lambda\le 20000/2147$ by the rigorous
upper bound of Section~\ref{sec:result}.

\emph{(iii) The $\mu_H$ are strictly increasing, hence pairwise distinct.}
The column-signature transfer matrix of the height-$\le H$ strip
(Section~\ref{sec:methodB}) is irreducible for $x>0$: from any signature
one reaches the single-cell signature (close all components with a
spanning column), and conversely any signature is reachable from it. The
height-$\le H$ system is a proper principal submatrix of the
height-$\le(H{+}1)$ system, connected to the new states through the cycle
(single cell) $\to$ (full column) $\to$ (single cell); deleting
rows and columns of an irreducible nonnegative matrix strictly decreases
its Perron root~\cite{hornjohnson}, so the strip growth constants strictly
increase. Strictness also rules out cancellation in the second
differences, so $1/\mu_H$ is a genuine pole of $G_H$ and is not a pole of
any $G_j$ with $j<H$. (Machine check: the ten exact denominators give
$\mu_H = 1,\ 1{+}\sqrt2,\ 3.4437,\dots,5.9917$, strictly increasing.)

\emph{(iv) Only finitely many algebraic integers have degree $\le D$ and
house $\le B$} (Kronecker~\cite{kronecker}): the minimal polynomial has
integer coefficients bounded by $\binom{D}{k}B^{k}$, leaving finitely many
possibilities. With $B=20000/2147$ and the infinitely many distinct
$\mu_H$ of (iii), it follows that $\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_H\to\infty$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:notdfinite}
$F(x,y)=\sum_{n,H}T(n,H)x^ny^H$ is not D-finite.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof.} Suppose $\sum_{i\le r}p_i(x,y)\,\partial_y^i F=0$ with
$p_i\in\mathbb Q[x,y]$, not all zero, and let $D=\max_i\deg_x p_i$.
Extracting $y$-coefficients gives, for every $N$, a relation
$\sum_{\delta}c_\delta(x,N)\,G_{N-\delta}(x)=0$ over a finite window of
$\delta$, where each $c_\delta(x,N)$ is polynomial in $N$ of degree $\le r$
and in $x$ of degree $\le D$, and where the $c_\delta$ jointly determine
the $p_i$ (falling factorials of $N$ are linearly independent). This
extraction is the opening move of Lemma~9 of~\cite{bmr2002}; what follows
is where we depart from it. Anchor the
window so that its top face is a level $H_0$: every other face refers to a
lower height, where $G$ is finite at $x=1/\mu_{H_0}$ by (iii), while
$G_{H_0}$ has a pole there, so the top coefficient vanishes at
$x=1/\mu_{H_0}$. A polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $\le D$ cannot
vanish at an algebraic number of degree exceeding $D$, so at every level
with $\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_{H_0}>D$ the top coefficient vanishes
identically in $x$; at $r{+}1$ such levels (which exist by (iv)) it
vanishes as a polynomial in $N$, i.e.\ the top face is identically zero.
The next face becomes the top and the same levels kill it in turn; all
faces vanish, so all $p_i=0$, a contradiction. $\square$

The abstract degree growth is visible concretely: the new-root contents of
the ten exact denominators have degrees $1,2,4,9,29,68,181,462,1254,3289$,
the first eight certified irreducible over $\mathbb Q$, so
$\deg_{\mathbb Q}\mu_8=462$ already. The argument uses only integer counts,
per-height rationality, and strictly increasing bounded strip constants, so
it applies verbatim to fixed polyominoes, polyhexes, and polyiamonds by
height.

\paragraph{Relation to existing work.}
Neither the conclusion nor the opening step is new. Non-D-finiteness of an
anisotropic generating function was proved for self-avoiding polygons, and
for directed bond animals, by Rechnitzer's haruspicy
programme~\cite{rechnitzer}; the criterion that programme runs on is
Lemma~9 of Bousquet-M\'elou and Rechnitzer~\cite{bmr2002}, whose proof
begins with the same coefficient extraction used above. From that shared
start the arguments diverge. Theirs concludes topologically: the union of
the coefficients' pole sets has finitely many limit points, via an
asymptotic argument in the index. Applying it demands an explicit
combinatorial description of the denominators, in the polygon setting a
product of cyclotomic polynomials. Ours concludes arithmetically and
effectively, following a single distinguished pole per slice with no inputs
beyond Perron--Frobenius monotonicity and a crude upper bound on $\lambda$.
That lightness is why the proof transports to any family with a column
transfer matrix, with no bespoke denominator analysis, and why it yields
explicit excluded $(r,D)$ boxes rather than a bare dichotomy. Arithmetic constraints on D-finite series do exist (the
\emph{D-finiteness, rationality and height} theorems of Bell, Nguyen and
Zannier~\cite{bnz2020} with their multivariate P\'olya--Carlson successor,
and the height-gap results of Bell, Hu and Satriano~\cite{bhs2020}), but
both bound the Weil heights of the \emph{coefficients} of a series, or of a
sequence read along the orbit of a rational map, and conclude rationality;
neither speaks about the growth constants of a family of slices. We have
located no application of either line to a lattice model, and no use of
Northcott finiteness on a family of slice growth constants; a literature
search cannot establish absence, so we claim the argument as ours with
that qualification.

One limitation. Klazar~\cite{klazar} enlarges the target
from D-finite to $D_A$-finite, analytic rather than polynomial
coefficients, on the ground that polynomiality was never carrying his
argument. Ours admits no such enlargement: the step ``a polynomial over
$\mathbb Q$ of degree $\le D$ cannot vanish at an algebraic number of degree
exceeding $D$'' \emph{is} the dichotomy. Theorem~\ref{thm:notdfinite} is
therefore about D-finiteness over $\mathbb Q(x)$ and does not reach
$D_A$-finiteness; the isotropic $a(x)$ remains open, as in the polygon
setting.

\paragraph{Rigorous lower bounds.}
Summing the fixed-height GFs gives $a(n)\ge\sum_{H\le 10}T(n,H)$ exactly for
any $n$, by extracting coefficients from rational functions. The bounds for
$a(25)$, $a(35)$ and $a(40)$ were each computed while that term was still
out of reach (the last of them
$a(40)\ge\num{4266005101622209395058618248135}$, an exact integer from
rational arithmetic), and all three are confirmed by the values Method~C
produced later, a retrospective certification of the GF machinery. The
capture fraction shrinks as taller strips dominate ($44\%$ of the exact
value at $n=25$, $7.5\%$ at $n=40$), but the bound remains available,
exactly, for any $n$ past the final computed term
(\texttt{paper/gf\_bound.py}).
