\begin{abstract}
\noindent
On the king lattice, the number $T(n,H)$ of fixed animals of $n$ cells with
bounding-box height exactly $H$ satisfies a diagonal law: for $k$ fixed and
$n \ge 2k+1$,
\[
  T(n, n-k) \;=\; P_k(n)\cdot 3^{\,n-1-3k},
\]
with $P_k$ a polynomial of degree $k$. The onset $n \ge 2k+1$ is sharp. This
paper is about the region below it, where the formula is false, and about the
use of the error there.

The formula is false by a vanishing relative amount, and the error has
structure that no algebraic search finds because the structure is analytic. Writing the defect at
depth $j = 2k+1-n$ as $\Dj(k)$, we report: an exact frame in which the entire
below-onset content of a column is $k+1$ integers; a closed form at depth $1$,
where the generating function is algebraic, an irreducible quartic, from
which the rate $9$, the exponent $-1/2$, the amplitude $\sqrt6/(27\sqrt\pi)$ and
the next coefficient $a = 3293/92928 - 3251\sqrt3/185856 \in \Q(\sqrt3)$ all
follow by singularity analysis; the same closure at depths $2$, $3$ and $4$; and
the measurement $\theta_j = j - 3/2$ with rate $9$ at every depth $j \le 7$.

The depth direction reduces to one constant. The seven measured amplitude ratios are not
seven numbers but one: the velocity $\alpha = 50/81$ at which the defect's
branch point moves when a cluster is allowed one unit of excess, computed exactly
by a finite local count. That resolves an ambiguity the measurements alone could
not --- at $j = 5$ the value is $35/8$, and the competing $118/27$, which fits
the numbers better, is an artifact of the normalization in which they were read.
The derivation of $\alpha$ rests on four analytic assumptions which we state
rather than prove.

Because the defect is computed from first principles, by an enumeration that
never reads the triangle, adding it back turns a \emph{short} entry into an
exact linear equation for a tall diagonal; a diagonal carries exactly two
constants, which is proved and formalized, so two such equations determine it.
That inverts the usual cost. The standard route reads a diagonal off the two
tallest entries on it, which are the most expensive computations in the
problem; this one reads it off entries already computed. Exact depths up to
$J$ give every diagonal attainable from $n \le 2H_{\max} + J - 1$, so each two
units of depth buy a height, and a height costs a factor of about $2.9$. In
practice it moved the two tallest entries of row $40$ inside existing data and
produced
\[
  a(41) \;=\; 393811462683918679824582849262105
\]
from heights $1$--$20$ enumerated directly, $4.7$ hours on 40 cores for the
first nineteen and $9.6$ hours on 76 for height $20$, against the $36.4$ hours
and $363$\,GB that row~$40$'s single tallest entry had cost on its own.

Finally, the construction is checked outside this project. On the square
lattice, where the counts are published by other people, a below-onset entry
corrected by its defect gives the same polynomial as the classical fit and
reproduces the tall entries it was denied, at both depths tested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The diagonal formula is an exact statement with an onset. Companion paper L1
proves that for a row-local lattice with drift $b$, the diagonal $T(H+k, H)$
equals $q_k(H)b^H$ with $\deg q_k \le k$, for $H$ past an explicit threshold,
and that the threshold is sharp: at one row below it the formula is wrong.
The king-lattice instance, in the indexing used throughout,
\begin{equation}
  T(n, n-k) \;=\; P_k(n)\cdot 3^{\,n-1-3k}, \qquad n \ge 2k+1,
  \label{eq:law}
\end{equation}
is exact above the onset line and false below it, and the region below the
onset line is the middle of the triangle, $H \le n/2$. We call the diagonal
$n - H = k$ \emph{level} $k$. Define the \emph{depth}
\[
  j \;=\; 2k+1-n \;\ge\; 1
\]
and the \emph{defect}
\[
  D_j(k) \;=\; T(n,n-k) - P_k(n)\cdot 3^{\,n-1-3k}, \qquad n = 2k+1-j .
\]
Depth $1$ is the first row the law misses, depth $2$ the next, and so on.

Two facts frame what follows. First, the formula is asymptotically exact where
it is false: the number of correct leading digits of $T$ grows with $k$ at every
fixed depth. Second, the defect has no algebraic structure that a search finds
directly --- C-finite to order $8$ and P-finite to $(r,d) = (4,4)$ both come back
null. The reason, established here, is that those searches were looking in the
wrong category and at the wrong scale: $D_1$ \emph{is} P-finite, at
$(r,d) = (35,4)$, and the structure that explains the whole family is a moving
branch point rather than a recurrence.

\paragraph{The error as an equation.} The classical way to determine $P_k$ is
to enumerate the two tallest entries of its diagonal, both above the onset, and
solve; those two entries are the most expensive computations in the whole
problem, because an enumeration reaches the tallest heights last and each
height costs a factor of about $2.9$. Since $\Dj(k)$ is computed by a program
that never reads the triangle, equation~\eqref{eq:pin} turns an entry
\emph{below} the onset into an exact linear equation for the same level,
obtained $j$ rows shorter. Section~\ref{sec:tower} is that construction, the
argument that it is not circular, and the term of the sequence it produced.

\paragraph{What this paper is not.} It is not a proof of the defect
asymptotics. Rate $9$ and exponent $j - 3/2$ are proved at $j = 1$ and measured
at $j \le 7$; the amplitude family is derived from an identity plus four
analytic assumptions that are stated in \S\ref{sec:assumptions} and proved
nowhere. The ledger on page 1 says which is which, claim by claim.

\section{The exact frame: a column is \texorpdfstring{$k+1$}{k+1} integers}
\label{sec:frame}

The chain identity behind \eqref{eq:law} gives, per column $k = n - H$,
\begin{equation}
  [y^k]F \;=\; \frac{R_k(z)}{(1-3z)^{\,k+1}}, \qquad R_k \in \Z[z], \quad
  \deg R_k \le 2k+1,
  \label{eq:column}
\end{equation}
and the step that produces the formula splits off a correction polynomial
$D(z)$ of degree at most $k$. Everything below the onset is that polynomial:
\begin{equation}
  D_j(k) \;=\; [z^{\,k+1-j}]\,D(z).
  \label{eq:defectcoeff}
\end{equation}
So the below-onset part of a column is $k+1$ integers rather than an infinite
tail to be estimated, and the depth-$j$ defect is one of them. In
particular the top one is a single coefficient of $R_k$:
\begin{equation}
  D_1(k) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{lead}(R_k)}{(-3)^{\,k+1}}.
  \label{eq:d1lead}
\end{equation}

The rest is computable because of this. A defect defined as a difference of two
large numbers would inherit their size; \eqref{eq:defectcoeff} makes it an object
in its own right, with its own generating function.

\begin{remark}[what the $k+1$ integers do not give]
A column below the onset holds $k+1$ integers, but each below-onset entry
brings one equation \emph{and} one unknown $D_j(k)$, so solving a whole column
is a reformulation rather than extra redundancy. The accounting is
\[
  \text{surplus} \;=\; \bigl(\text{entries whose depth has a known } D_j\bigr) - 2,
\]
and an entry at a depth whose defect is unknown contributes nothing. Nor do
the integers suffice to fit a recurrence for $D_j$ in $k$: a recurrence of the
orders in question needs on the order of $180$ values of $k$, and about $15$
exist.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Two constants per level, and one equation per short entry}
\label{sec:pin}

\paragraph{Two constants.} The grand form
(\repo{docs/proofs/grand-form.md}) states that the diagonal generating function
factorizes as $F(n,u) = C(u)\cdot H(u)^n$, from which level $k$ carries exactly
two new constants over the levels below it. Any two independent linear
equations in those constants therefore determine $P_k$ completely.

\paragraph{One equation per short entry.} Define the defect at depth
$j = 2k+1-n$ by the difference between the true entry and the formula:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pin}
  T(2k+1-j,\; k+1-j) \;=\; P_k(2k+1-j)\cdot 3^{\,2k-3k-j} \;+\; \Dj(k).
\end{equation}
As an identity this is a definition rearranged; it introduces no approximation
and no asymptotics. It becomes \emph{useful} exactly when $\Dj(k)$ is known by
some route that does not involve $P_k$, and then it is one linear equation in
the two unknown constants of level $k$, obtained at an entry that is $j$ rows
shorter than the two classical ones.

Two such equations, at two depths or two levels, determine the level.

\section{Depth one closes}
\label{sec:depth1}

\subsection{The defect without the triangle}

Equation~\eqref{eq:d1lead} makes $D_1(k)$ computable without $P_k$: the
leading coefficient of $R_k$ assembles from the all-pairs cluster families
alone, by the gap walk of companion paper L1 (a walk over the gaps between
consecutive rows), with no triangle data and no $P_k$ anywhere. Depth one is
therefore exact to $k = 200$ in about $40$ seconds.

\subsection{The generating function is algebraic}

The generating function $W(x) = \sum_k D_1(k)x^k$ satisfies an irreducible
quartic $\Phi(x, W) = 0$, derived by the kernel method: the kernel
$u^2 - y(1+u+u^2)^2$ factors over $\Q(\sqrt y)$, the two small roots and the
analyticity conditions they impose pin the boundary unknowns, and the degree-$4$
norm of the resulting expression is $\Phi$ coefficient for coefficient. No series
data enters the derivation. As a check, $\Phi$ annihilates all $200$ computed
orders, and a modular scan shows no smaller box does --- no cubic, no quadratic,
nothing rational, within the degrees the series supports.

$D_1$ is also P-finite, at $(r,d) = (35,4)$, which is why searches at
envelopes $(4,4)$ and $(6,8)$ found nothing.

\subsection{Every depth-one constant, derived}

From $\Phi$, singularity analysis~\cite[Ch.~VI--VII]{flajoletSedgewick2009}
gives the constants; $\Phi$ is algebraic
with a square-root branch point, which is the case
\cite[Thm.~VII.8]{flajoletSedgewick2009} covers, and the $k^{-1/2}$ and the
$1/k$ correction are read off the Puiseux expansion there:
\[
  D_1(k) \;=\; C_1\,9^k\,k^{-1/2}\Bigl(1 + \frac{a}{k} + O(k^{-2})\Bigr),
  \qquad
  C_1 = \frac{\sqrt6}{27\sqrt\pi},
\]
\[
  a \;=\; \frac{3293}{92928} - \frac{3251\sqrt3}{185856}
    \;=\; 0.005138939956706\dots
\]
The rate $9$ and the exponent $-1/2$ are branch data of $\Phi$. The amplitude
matches the measured $0.05118432$ within a control-backed bar of $3\times10^{-7}$
relative, and the recognition is unique: over all $\sqrt m/(n\sqrt\pi)$ with
$m \le 200$, $n \le 400$, exactly one value lies within $10^{-6}$ of the
measurement.

The coefficient $a$ lies in $\Q(\sqrt3)$, which is why searches for it over
the rationals, or over products of $\sqrt6$ and $\pi$, found nothing.

\subsection{Onset sharpness}
\label{sec:sharp3}

$D_1(k) \ne 0$ is the non-cancellation statement that makes the diagonal
formula's onset sharp. It holds for every $k \ge 1$. Put $N(x) = \sum_{k \ge 1} N_k x^k$
with $N_k = 3^{k+1}D_1(k) = 3^{k+1}T(2k,k) - P_k(2k)$, an integer series ($P_k$
has degree $k$ and takes the integer values $T(n,n-k)\,3^{3k+1-n}$ at the $k+1$
consecutive integers $n = 2k+1, \dots, 3k+1$, hence integer values on all of
$\mathbb{Z}$), annihilated by the derived $\Phi$ after the substitution $y = 3x$,
$W = 3W_1 + 1$. Reduce modulo $3$. The quartic factors:
\[
  \Phi(x,W) \;\equiv\; 2\,(W-1)^3\bigl((1+x)W - x\bigr)
  \qquad\text{in } \mathbb{F}_3[x,W].
\]
$\mathbb{F}_3[[x]]$ is an integral domain, so the reduction of $N$ kills one
factor; $W \equiv 1$ would force $N_1 \equiv 0$, and $N_1 = 4$. The reduction is
therefore $x/(1+x)$, so $N_k \equiv (-1)^{k+1} \pmod 3$, so $N_k \ne 0$, for
every $k \ge 1$. The same congruence gives the denominator of $D_1(k)$ in
lowest terms: exactly $3^{k+1}$.

The argument rests on the identity of \S\ref{sec:depth1} expressing $D_1$
through the all-pairs cluster families, derived from the chain identity and
machine-verified at $19$ exact cells, but not written out as a standalone
proof.

\section{The depth direction is one constant}
\label{sec:ridge}

\subsection{Depth is a coupling, not a new object}

Under $Y = yz$ and $t = 1/z$, the proved chain identity collapses to
\begin{equation}
  G(Y,t) \;=\; \widehat P(Y,t) + \frac{\widehat B(Y,t)^2}{\,t - 3 - \widehat S(Y,t)\,},
  \qquad
  D_j(k) = [Y^k t^{\,j-1}]\,G ,
  \label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
valid for $j - 1 \le k$, where $\widehat S, \widehat B, \widehat P$ are the
excess-graded aggregates of the cluster families and $t$ marks excess. The
grading is legitimate because a family of excess $e$ carries $k \ge e+1$, so
$t^e$ always arrives with at least $Y^{e+1}$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:master} reproduces the depth-$1$ through depth-$4$ series at
all $74$ exact entries with $k \le 19$, and perturbing one excess-$1$ weight by
$+1$ breaks it. It identifies the depth variable as a marker for cluster
excess, so depth is a perturbation of the row transfer of the gap walk, a
coupling constant rather than a new object to be summed.

\subsection{A moving branch point}

In the normalization $D_j(k) \sim C_j 9^k k^{j-3/2}$ the family has one scaling
variable $\tau = t/(1-9Y)$, and the family of amplitude ratios becomes
\[
  \frac{C_{M+1}}{C_1} \;=\; \frac{\alpha^M}{M!},
\]
a pure exponential rather than a family of binomials. An exponential enhancement
per unit chain length is exactly a square-root branch point whose \emph{location}
moves linearly with the excess marker,
\[
  1 - 9Y_c(t) \;=\; \alpha t + O(t^2),
\]
with the exponent fixed at $-1/2$. So $\alpha$ is a local quantity: the
first-order shift of the zero-momentum eigenvalue of the gap walk's row transfer
under one $3$-cell row. That is a finite count, and it is
\[
  \alpha \;=\; \frac{\Sigma}{729} \;=\; \frac{450}{729} \;=\; \frac{50}{81}.
\]

\subsection{What the measurements could not decide}

In the normalization $D_j(k) \sim C_j 9^k k^{j-3/2}$ read without the exponential,
the amplitude ratios are
$1, 1, 3/2, 5/2, 4.370843, 7.866341, 14.441622$ against the candidate
$\binom{2j-2}{j-1}/2^{j-1} = 1, 1, 3/2, 5/2, 35/8, 63/8, 231/16$. At $j = 3$ and
$j = 4$ the candidate is the only rational with denominator $\le 32$ inside the
measurement bar. At $j = 5$ it is not: eight simple rationals fit, and one of
them, $118/27 = 4.370370$, is \emph{closer} to the measurement than
$35/8 = 4.375$.

The derivation settles it. With $C_{M+1}/C_1 = \alpha^M/M!$ exactly and
$\alpha = 50/81$, the value at $j = 5$ is $35/8$. The measurement returns
$118/27$ when it is read in a normalization that hides the exponential, so that
number is an artifact of the reading rather than a competing constant.

\subsection{The four assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

The derivation of $\alpha$ is conditional on the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The exponent stays exactly $-1/2$ for small $t > 0$.} The dispersion
  has a nondegenerate stationary point at $u = 1$, with $(\log K)''(1) = 4/3$
  symbolically, and analytic perturbations of a nondegenerate minimum stay
  nondegenerate. What is missing is control of the step from the dispersion to
  the assembled $G$.
\item \emph{$Y_c(t)$ is analytic at $t = 0$.} Each order in $t$ adds finitely
  many excess families, so the vertex expansion is order-by-order finite.
  Convergence is not proved.
\item \emph{The cancellation that keeps $1/9$ dominant persists at every order in
  $t$.} Rate $9$ is measured at every depth $j \le 6$ and derived at $j = 1$
  only.
\item \emph{The perturbative bookkeeping behind $\alpha = \Sigma/729$} --- a flat
  zero-momentum projection inside the delocalized sector. The excursion lands in the well-behaved sector
  $450$ times out of $450$ and in the badly-behaved one never, so the vertex acts
  where the transfer is a translation-invariant walk of mass $9$ and the flat
  mode is the critical mode. What remains unproved is the passage from
  ``the zero-momentum mass shifts by $Y^2t\,\Sigma/9$'' to ``the branch point of
  $G$ moves by $\alpha t$ with its exponent intact'', which is assumption 1 in
  other clothes.
\end{enumerate}

Two further items are verified rather than derived. $\alpha_M$ is constant over
$M = 1,2,3$ within calibrated bias --- sharp at $M=1$ ($1.1\times10^{-9}$),
useful at $M=2$, and at $M=3$ constraining almost nothing on its own. And the
asymptotics at $j = 5,6,7$ are a prediction: no exact $D_5$ beyond $k \le 19$
exists.

\section{The near-onset layer}
\label{sec:crossover}

Close to the onset line a resummation of the family,
\[
  D_j(k) \;\approx\; \frac{\sqrt6}{27}\cdot\frac{\binom{2N}{N}}{4^N}
  \binom{N}{k}9^k\Bigl(\frac{50}{81}\Bigr)^{j-1}, \qquad N = k+j-1,
\]
is accurate and degrades smoothly with depth, which implies a scaling variable.
Fitting the residual surface over $168$ computed below-onset entries finds a boundary
layer of width $j \sim k^{0.4}$.

That exponent, and the crossover curve read off it, were downgraded on
adversarial review and are reported here as measurement with the weaker status
they came back with; the family exclusions from the same analysis stand. The
resummation is a near-onset statement and not a uniform description of the
below-onset region.

\section{Determining a level from below its onset}
\label{sec:tower}

The defect makes a short entry into an equation, and two equations determine
a level.

\subsection{The defect cannot see the answer}

$\Dj(k)$ comes from bounded-excess cluster-weight families: an enumeration over
families with a bounded excess parameter, carried out by a dynamic program that
reads neither the triangle $T$ nor any $P_k$: it is the enumeration of
\S\ref{sec:depth1} and \S\ref{sec:depths}, which close depths $1$ through $4$.

The separation is enforced by a check that fails if the two sides are ever
joined, and the script that solves for the constants carries three negative
controls, deliberately corrupted inputs it must reject, and does: a perturbed
$\Dj$ breaks the solution; the same equation offered twice is refused as
singular; a corrupted lower level breaks the solution.

\paragraph{From the adversarial review.} The review of this construction
(\repo{results/undertow.md} and its siblings) traced every input to
its source, and its most useful finding is this:

\begin{quote}
Two routes through the formulas agreeing is \emph{not} two confirmations.
Every formula statement about $T(40,19)$ contains $D_3(21)$, and the two
routes share the grand form and most of the defect values. Only agreement
against an enumeration crosses assumption families.
\end{quote}

The audit reported in \S\ref{sec:reach} is therefore a consistency check on
the computed triangle, not an independent confirmation of it. What crosses
assumption families is the second program (a different counting rule, at
heights $1$--$19$ of the row; the formulas above are refitted to its entries),
the direct enumeration of $T(41,20)$ (\S\ref{sec:limits}), and the external
validation of \S\ref{sec:square}.

\subsection{Reach, and what it bought}
\label{sec:reach}

With exact defects to depth $J$, the construction covers every level
reachable from
\[
  n \;\le\; 2\,H_{\max} + J - 1 .
\]
Each two units of depth buy one height, and a height is a factor of about $2.9$
in compute.

\paragraph{Row 40, with no new computation.} $P_{19}$ is determined by
$T(37,18)$ and $T(36,17)$; $P_{20}$ by $T(38,18)$ and $T(37,17)$. All four
entries lie inside rows already computed at $H \le 18$, so $T(40,20)$ and
$T(40,21)$ became predictions from existing data rather than computations.

\paragraph{$a(41)$.} Computed in two halves (\repo{results/a41/PROVENANCE.md}).
Heights $1$--$19$ were enumerated directly on 2026-08-20, $4.72$ hours on 40
cores with peak memory $557$\,MB, and height $20$ on 2026-09-05, $9.6$ hours on
76 cores; heights $21$--$41$ come from the formulas, with $P_k$ for $k \le 19$
from the program's table and $P_{20}$ determined from below-onset entries. The
result is
\[
  a(41) \;=\; 393811462683918679824582849262105 .
\]
The comparison worth making is against row $40$'s single tallest entry:
$36.4$ hours on 32 cores and a $363$\,GB disk peak, for one entry. Row $41$
did not pay it.

\paragraph{The audit.} Depths $1$--$4$ together with the two classical
equations give six equations for two unknowns at each level, so four
independent consistency checks per level, computed from short entries through
mathematics that shares nothing with the enumeration. All $18$ formulas in the
program's table were re-derived exactly over every available depth pair: $18$
of $18$. As \S\ref{sec:tower} says, this checks the computed values against
each other and the frame; it is not a second enumeration.

\section{The square lattice}
\label{sec:square}

Every second program in this repository re-counts the same objects with
different code, so a shared \emph{conceptual} error would survive all of them.
The square lattice does not have that defect as a check, because its counts
are published by other people.

There the drift set has $b = |D| = 1$, so the $3^{\,n-1-3k}$ factor is $1$ and
the law is a plain polynomial $T_{\mathrm{sq}}(n, n-k) = P_k(n)$ of degree $k$
for $n \ge 2k+1$. The depth-$1$ defect, measured on the computed square
bounding-box triangle, is
\[
  D_1(k) \;=\; (-1)^{\,k+1},
  \qquad k = 1,\dots,6 .
\]

\paragraph{Depth one.} Fit each $P_k$ twice: classically, from the $k+1$
above-onset entries at $n = 2k+1, \dots, 3k+1$; and by dropping the
\emph{tallest} of those and using the depth-$1$ entry at $n = 2k$ in its place.
At every level $k = 1,\dots,6$ the two fits are equal as polynomials, and the
second reproduces the tall entry it was denied. A negative control confirms
that the wrong defect sign breaks the fit.

\paragraph{Depth two.} Dropping the two tallest above-onset entries and using
$n = 2k-1$ and $n = 2k$ instead gives exactly $k+1$ points for a degree-$k$
polynomial, with the two tallest entries never seen. At $k = 2,\dots,6$ the
fit again agrees with the classical one and predicts both withheld entries
exactly: ten withheld entries, ten exact hits, in exact rational arithmetic. A
$D_2$ corrupted by $1$ breaks the fit at every $k$; without that second
control the whole table would be consistent with $D_2$ being irrelevant.

\paragraph{$D_2$ has no closed form in five levels.} The measured values are
\[
  D_2(k) \;=\; -4,\; 8,\; -3,\; 10,\; -1
  \qquad (k = 2,\dots,6).
\]
The sign alternates as $(-1)^{k+1}$, like $D_1$. The magnitudes $4, 8, 3, 10, 1$
are not monotone, do not separate by parity, and support no fit worth writing
down on three and two points. They are recorded as five values.

\paragraph{The limit.} These square defects are
measured \emph{against} a $P_k$ that was determined classically. That is
exactly what is needed to validate the identity, that an entry below the
onset, corrected, is a valid equation for the same polynomial, but it is not an
independent
route to $D_j$ on the square lattice, and so it does not by itself make a square
$P_k$ cheaper. On the king lattice the corresponding defects come from the
family enumeration of \S\ref{sec:depth1} and \S\ref{sec:depths}, which never
sees $P_k$. Making that enumeration lattice-parametric is a build and not a
substitution: its row transfer has king adjacency baked in, and the
lattice-parametric machinery this project has elsewhere covers the
\emph{above}-onset assembly only.

\section{Novelty}
\label{sec:novelty}

Two priority passes ran over this material, on the two halves separately.

\subsection{The defect mathematics: no collision, and a weak negative}


A pass ran on 2026-08-18 and found nothing close
(\repo{docs/priority-passes-2026-08-18.md}): searches over corrections and error
terms to fixed-height and bounding-box animal formulas, asymptotics of defects
below a formula's threshold, and algebraic generating functions for the leading
coefficients of such families.

That negative is weak. The object here is defined relative to a diagonal
formula of our own, so a collision would have to be with work that had the
formula first, which companion paper L1's searches cover, and they came back
clean. Nothing here is called new.

\subsection{The construction: a near neighbor}

A pass ran on 2026-08-23 and found a near neighbor.

\paragraph{The prior art.} Correcting a finite-size calculation by a term that
describes how far it is wrong, in order to use it beyond the range where it is
exact, is standard practice in series work on directed percolation. Baxter and
Guttmann~\cite{baxter1988} extend the percolation-probability series for the
directed square lattice by exactly that maneuver: a finite-lattice calculation
of size $N$ is exact only to some order, the differences between the exact
series and the finite ones are \emph{correction terms}, and knowing them buys
series orders that the finite lattice alone does not reach. Jensen and
Guttmann~\cite{jensen1995} carry it further on the square, honeycomb and
triangular lattices. The ambient family is the finite-lattice method of Enting
and of Jensen. This paper is a member of that family and does not claim the
shape of the idea.

\paragraph{What differs.} In that
literature the correction terms are \emph{conjectured and fitted} --- Baxter and
Guttmann's ansatz makes them rational functions of Catalan numbers --- so the
extended series is a prediction resting on the ansatz holding at the next
order. Two things differ here.

\begin{itemize}
\item The correction is computed exactly and independently. $D_j(k)$
comes from a bounded-excess family enumeration that never reads the triangle
and never reads $P_k$ (\S\ref{sec:tower}); it is not fitted to the object it
corrects, and it carries no ansatz.
\item The number of unknowns per level is proved, not assumed. The grand form
gives exactly two constants per level, so ``two equations suffice'' is a
theorem rather than a working hypothesis.
\end{itemize}

Together those make the output an exact value with a provenance rather than an
extrapolation with an error estimate. This paper puts forward that combination
and not the idea of correcting finite-size data.

\paragraph{The negative half.} No instance was found that applies a correction
of this kind to bounding-box-height animal counts, or that corrects a diagonal
formula in order to determine its levels from below the onset. That search was web only;
the record of what was and was not searched is
\repo{docs/priority-pass-L10-2026-08-23.md}.

\section{Open problems}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{openproblem}[prove the defect asymptotics]
Rate $9$ and exponent $j - 3/2$ are derived at $j = 1$ from the quartic and
measured at $j \le 7$. A proof at general $j$ needs the four assumptions of
\S\ref{sec:assumptions}, of which item 4 is self-contained: redo the first-order
perturbation with the correct left and right critical vectors of the transfer and
confirm that $\Sigma/729$ falls out.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[an exact $D_5$]
Every statement about $j \ge 5$ in this paper is a prediction, because the exact
data stops at $k \le 19$ there. Extending the excess-graded family enumeration by
one unit of excess would test the $35/8$ against something other than its own
derivation.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[algebraicity at every depth]
$D_1$ is algebraic. Is $D_2$? The master identity gives each depth as a
coefficient of one bivariate object, so the natural question is whether $G$ is
algebraic in $Y$ at every fixed order in $t$, which the depth-$1$ quartic
establishes only at $t^0$.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}
Make the family DP lattice-parametric. The above-onset half of the ledger
already is: the cumulant assembly carries over to any row-local lattice by the
single substitution $3 \mapsto b = |D|$. The below-onset half does not, because
adjacency is baked into the family DP's row transfer. Doing so would give
$\Dj$ on the square lattice at every depth and turn the external check from a
validation into a second instance.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}
Is there a closed form for the square-lattice $D_2$? Five levels of exact values
produced none, which is the first place the defect's structure has looked
different away from the king lattice.
\end{openproblem}
