# r4-gen4 — plan

**Question, one sentence.** If every live route of round 4 lands, what does a
skeptical referee still not accept, which of tonight's instruments retarget to
the project's *other* sequences for free, and what is the cheapest remaining
confirmation bit anywhere in the campaign?

**Why this and not my predecessors'.** r4-gen worked the consequences of the
band geometry and the diagonal law; r4-gen2 worked the cost model and the
independence surface; r4-gen3 worked the plumbing already on disk. All three
are pointed at *this round's* measurements. I am pointed at the state of the
world *after* the round succeeds, and at what "succeeded" would mean.

## Steps, in order

1. Read the round-4 brief, the full queue, the r3 synthesis closed doors and
   Three floors. (produces: no file; the constraint that no row repeats one)
2. Measure the height shares of a(40) off `results/triangle.txt` so the
   three-epistemic-states accounting is arithmetic, not recollection.
   (produces: a table in the deliverable §1)
3. Angle 1 — the residue after total success. Enumerate what stays unproved
   when H=15..16 exact, H=17..19 mod p, H=20..21 mod 2 and the Lean sufficiency
   theorem all land; file rows against that residue.
4. Angle 2 — retargetability. Index the project's other sequences
   (`results/related-seqs-*`, perimeter, holes, convex, directed) and score
   each of the four instruments (B1 colour DP, spin engine, flood-fill oracle,
   Lean recurrence) on what it costs to point at them.
5. Angle 3 — bits per thread-hour. Rank every unclaimed confirmation, including
   the unglamorous ones; name the single best next thread-hour and defend it
   against "that is not the frontier".
6. Second pass: what my own rows share.
7. Append rows to `results/r4/queue.md` (append only), file the deliverable.

**What would make me stop.** Nothing short of the lead stopping me — standing
instruction is never to stop making ideas. If an angle collapses I file the
kill and move to the next.

**Hard constraints.** No compute on any machine, nothing on gympie beyond
reading files. Read-only ssh only. No `/tmp`, no `pkill`/`pgrep`.
