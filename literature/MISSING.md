# Papers wanted and not held

Papers cited or relied on here that `literature/` does not hold. Each was
searched for from the desk (title search, arXiv, the authors' pages); what is
left is a library, the author, or nothing. Remote e-access is not sold to
unaffiliated readers; Penn State lends to Pennsylvania residents; pre-1995
IOP content is a separate archive product, so a current licence does not
imply it. Remove an entry, and file the PDF, once obtained.

## High

- G. E. Andrews, "Generalized Frobenius partitions," Memoirs AMS 301 (1984).
  ISBN 0821823027. The product form of φ₂ (A053993), which
  `results/perimeter.md` cites as Andrews eq. (5.9), is quoted from the OEIS
  entry and has never been read; the product was checked against the cone
  count to n = 40, so nothing numerical is at risk. The memoir is also where
  φ_k is defined, so it is the place to learn whether "order ideals of the
  cone {a ≥ |b|}" is a known reading of φ₂. `ams.org/memo/0301` returns an
  access page, not the PDF.
- G. Barequet & B. Magal, "Automatic generation of formulae for polyominoes
  with a fixed perimeter defect," Comput. Geom. 108 (2023) 101919. The
  perimeter-defect member of the same family as the height-defect diagonals,
  with an algorithm that emits closed formulae per defect, k ≤ 5. Prior art
  for the statement shape; worth reading to see whether it subsumes the
  defect bookkeeping here. No preprint; the author's page offers a reprint
  request by mail.
- E. J. Janse van Rensburg & N. Madras, "Metropolis Monte Carlo simulation
  of lattice animals," J. Phys. A 30 (1997) 8035–8066. Madras 1999 points at
  its §2.4 and §2.8 for applications of the pattern theorem, so if anyone has
  already stated that simply-connected animals are exponentially rare, it is
  there; and a Metropolis chain on animals must establish its own
  irreducibility, so the connectivity of the single-cell move graph (idea 8 of
  `results/closed-doors.md`) is probably a lemma in it. Check before anything
  in `results/subclasses.md` is called new.

## Medium

- P. J. Peard & D. S. Gaunt, "1/d-expansions for the free energy of lattice
  animal models of a self-interacting branched polymer," J. Phys. A 28 (1995)
  6109–6124. Its eq. (2.15), p. 6113, is where the polynomial-times-exponential
  diagonal shape was first predicted, before Barequet–Shalah proved it for
  polycubes; the earliest citation for the statement shape.
- N. Madras, "A rigorous bound on the critical exponent for the number of
  lattice trees, animals, and polygons," J. Stat. Phys. 78 (1995) 681–699.
  Abstract: θ ≥ (d−1)/d, so θ ≥ ½ in 2D, against the −1 estimated here.
- N. Madras, C. E. Soteros & S. G. Whittington, "Statistics of lattice
  animals," J. Phys. A 21 (1988) 4617–4635. The classical rigorous-statistics
  paper; the place to check what about subclasses is already proved.
- N. Madras & G. Slade, *The Self-Avoiding Walk*, Birkhäuser 1993. Madras
  1999 proves its ratio theorem by reusing Theorem 7.3.2 here. A book.
- E. J. Janse van Rensburg & S. G. Whittington, "Punctured discs on the
  square lattice," J. Phys. A 23 (1990) 1287–1294. The nearest prior art on
  holes; `results/subclasses.md` says no paper measures hole area, and this
  is the one that could contradict it.
- B. M. I. Rands & D. J. A. Welsh, "Animals, trees and renewal sequences,"
  IMA J. Appl. Math. 27 (1981) 1–18. The renewal identity over
  inconstructible animals; if stated lattice-generically it gives polyplets
  the same identity and Bui's conditional upper-bound machinery.
- G. Barequet & G. Ben-Shachar, "Algorithms for counting minimum-perimeter
  lattice animals," Algorithmica 85 (2023) 75–99, doi
  10.1007/s00453-022-01008-9. The tool for extending the king
  minimum-perimeter sequence without brute force. No arXiv version.
- F. Ibn-Majdoub-Hassani, "Combinatoire des polyominos et des tableaux
  décalés oscillants," thesis, Paris-Sud 1996, `theses.fr/1996PA112406`,
  no digitised text. Reference [6] of Gouyou-Beauchamps & Leroux 2004 and
  the one candidate origin of the block decomposition L5 uses.
- A. R. Conway, "Enumerating 2D percolation series by the finite-lattice
  method: theory," J. Phys. A 28 (1995) 335–349; A. R. Conway & A. J.
  Guttmann, "On two-dimensional percolation," J. Phys. A 28 (1995) 891–904;
  I. G. Enting, "Generating functions for enumerating self-avoiding rings on
  the square lattice," J. Phys. A 13 (1980) 3713. The finite-lattice method,
  whose bounding-box decomposition the four-direction split here sits
  inside. Lineage only; nothing depends on reading them.
- A. R. Conway, R. Brak & A. J. Guttmann, "Directed animals on
  two-dimensional lattices," J. Phys. A 26 (1993) 3085–3091. Only to see
  whether its lattice list includes the king case.

## Low

- I. G. Enting & A. J. Guttmann, "On the area of square lattice polygons,"
  J. Stat. Phys. 58 (1990) 475–484. Was wanted as the primary for the
  bond-perimeter maximum; Fisher, Guttmann & Whittington 1991 shows that
  statement is folklore, so probably nothing to find.
- D. J. Kleitman, M. M. Krieger & B. L. Rothschild, Studies in Appl. Math. 50
  (1971); B. Bollobás & I. Leader, Combinatorica 11 (1991) 299–314 and
  European J. Combin. 11 (1990) 325–340; S. L. Bezrukov, "An isoperimetric
  problem for Manhattan lattices." The edge-isoperimetric companions to the
  vertex result cited from Wang & Wang 1977; needed only if the bond-perimeter
  twin is ever printed.
- D. J. Klein, "Rigorous results for branched polymers with excluded volume,"
  J. Chem. Phys. 75 (1981) 5186–5189. The other route to existence of the
  growth constant; Klarner is held.
- D. S. Gaunt & P. J. Peard, "1/d-expansions for the free energy of weakly
  embedded site animal models," J. Phys. A 33 (2000) 7515–7539.
- F. Harary & H. Harborth, "Extremal animals," J. Comb. Inf. Syst. Sci. 1
  (1976) 1–8. The likeliest place a king-lattice perimeter statement already
  exists.
- B. Delyon, thesis, Paris VI 1980. The cluster-size result behind the
  Hammond and Georgakopoulos–Panagiotis chain; probably unobtainable.

Free on arXiv and not yet filed: Hsu & Grassberger, physics/9911023, and
Hsu, Nadler & Grassberger, cond-mat/0408061, which apply PERM sampling to
lattice animals and reach square-lattice site animals to n = 46 with error
bars. The sampling literature is not missing, only unfiled.

## Searches on record

**The non-D-finiteness argument** (`results/anisotropic-not-dfinite.md`,
L4): does "slice growth constants strictly increasing and bounded, hence
slice degrees unbounded by Northcott, hence not D-finite" appear anywhere?
The opening move (extract y-coefficients from the ODE to get a recurrence) is
Bousquet-Mélou & Rechnitzer 2002, Lemma 9, which both Haruspicy papers cite
for it; their conclusion is topological (pole accumulation), this one is
arithmetic. Forward citations of the seeds were read in three databases:

| seed | OpenAlex | Semantic Scholar | Google Scholar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bousquet-Mélou & Rechnitzer 2002 | 83 | 74 | 79 |
| Haruspicy 2 | 13 | 17 | 14 |
| Haruspicy 3 | 4 | 4 | |
| Chan & Rechnitzer 2018 | 20 | 2 | |
| Bevan, Brignall, Elvey Price & Pantone 2020 | 3 | 20 | |

Scholar's full-text search within the 79 citing papers for "Northcott" and
"Mahler" returned 0 hits each. Nearest relatives, none a collision: Bell,
Nguyen & Zannier, Trans. AMS 373 (2020), which bounds heights of coefficients
rather than of slice growth constants (now cited in L4); Bell, Gerhold,
Klazar & Luca 2008 and Gerhold 2004 (analytic, elementary-function
sequences); Bell, Hu & Satriano, arXiv 2003.01255 (arithmetic dynamics).
Absence cannot be established by search, and the papers say so.
`experiments/citation_crawl.py` reruns the two API crawls.

**The maximum hole area** (`results/subclasses.md`): the primary for the
grid isoperimetric inequality is Wang & Wang, SIAM J. Appl. Math. 32 (1977)
860–870, for arbitrary sets with no connectivity hypothesis; Sieben 2008 and
Altshuler et al. 2006 supply the closed forms. The bond-perimeter twin
(m²/16) is stated as "clearly" in both Prellberg & Owczarek 1999 and Fisher,
Guttmann & Whittington 1991 and has no primary.

**The perimeter-defect theorems** (L6): the Asinowski–Barequet–Zheng slides
(Séminaire Lotharingien 79, `mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/wpapers/s79vortrag/`) and
their ANALCO 2018 paper (held) carry the k = e + 2f identity and the
rational-with-cyclotomic-denominator theorem for fixed defect; L6 attributes
both in place.

**Concatenation bounds** (Barequet, Ben-Shachar & Osegueda 2021, held): the
method bounds growth constants, not log-convexity, and a king comb shatters
under their lexicographic split into about n/4 components, so it does not
give an upper bound below 9.3154 (`experiments/concatenation_bound_check.py`).
