Non-D-finiteness of anisotropic generating functions (the lineage our theorem sits in):
  bousquet-melou_rechnitzer_2002_lattice_animals_heaps_of_dimers.pdf
                                           Bousquet-Melou & Rechnitzer, Lattice animals and heaps of
                                           dimers, Discrete Math. 258 (2002) 235-274. **Lemma 9 is the
                                           source criterion**: if S(q,u) is D-finite in u with coefficients
                                           rational in q, the union of the coefficients' pole sets has
                                           finitely many limit points. Both Haruspicy papers cite this,
                                           not Haruspicy 1, for the D-finiteness test. Free from
                                           labri.fr/perso/bousquet (PostScript; converted here).
  rechnitzer_2006_haruspicy2_sap_not_dfinite.pdf
                                           Rechnitzer, Haruspicy 2: the anisotropic GF of self-avoiding
                                           polygons is not D-finite, JCTA 113 (2006) 520-546
                                           (arXiv math/0406450). Criterion restated as Theorem 15.
  rechnitzer_2006_haruspicy3_bond_animals_not_dfinite.pdf
                                           Rechnitzer, Haruspicy 3: the directed bond-animal GF is not
                                           D-finite, JCTA (2006) (arXiv math/0408054). Same criterion as
                                           Theorem 5.
  rechnitzer_2003_haruspicy1_anisotropic_gf.pdf
                                           Rechnitzer, Haruspicy and anisotropic generating functions,
                                           Adv. in Appl. Math. 30 (2003) 228-257. Obtained 2026-08-01
                                           (jasonp, ScienceDirect). Supplies the combinatorial
                                           section/density machinery -- for a wide range of bond-animal
                                           families the coefficient of y^n is rational, numerator degree
                                           <= denominator degree, denominator a product of cyclotomic
                                           polynomials, with a tight multiplicative upper bound. NOT the
                                           source of the D-finiteness test (that is BM-R 2002, above).
                                           Section 2 explains the name: haruspicy is divination by
                                           examining the organs of animals, and the technique examines
                                           bond configurations inside lattice animals.
  klazar_2003_non_p_recursiveness_matchings.pdf
                                           Klazar, Non-P-recursiveness of numbers of matchings (linear
                                           chord diagrams) with many crossings, Adv. in Appl. Math. 30
                                           (2003) 126-136. Same issue; cited by Haruspicy 2 as [13].
                                           Adjacent prior art on proving sequences non-P-recursive.
                                           Obstruction is ANALYTIC (divergence); introduces D_A-finite.
  bell_gerhold_klazar_luca_2008_nonholonomicity_elementary_functions.pdf
                                           Bell, Gerhold, Klazar & Luca, Non-holonomicity of sequences
                                           defined via elementary functions, Ann. Comb. (2008),
                                           arXiv math/0605142. Analytic method (counting zeros); class is
                                           elementary functions evaluated at integers. Swept 2026-08-01
                                           as part of closing the Northcott novelty item; no collision.

Grid isoperimetry (the source of the maxhole formula, results/maxhole-proof.md).
All obtained 2026-08-06; the backward pass through the two 2006/2008 reference
lists is what turned up the 1977 primary:
  wang_wang_1977_discrete_isoperimetric_problems.pdf
                                           Wang & Wang, Discrete Isoperimetric Problems, SIAM J. Appl.
                                           Math. 32(4) (1977) 860-870. THE PRIMARY. A linear ordering of
                                           Z^n whose every prefix minimizes the boundary (= points not in
                                           the set at Euclidean distance 1, i.e. the 4-neighbourhood)
                                           among sets of that size. Arbitrary sets, no connectivity. The
                                           minimizers are "standard spheres" = |x|+|y| <= m plus part of
                                           the next shell: our diagonal diamonds. Equivalent to
                                           Macaulay's theorem, which they also show.
  bollobas_radcliffe_1990_isoperimetric_cube_grid.pdf
                                           Bollobas & Radcliffe, Isoperimetric Inequalities for Faces of
                                           the Cube and the Grid, European J. Combin. 11 (1990) 323-333.
                                           Altshuler's [4]; the combinatorics-side reference.
  chung_2004_discrete_isoperimetric_inequalities.pdf
                                           Chung, Discrete Isoperimetric Inequalities, Surveys in Diff.
                                           Geom. IX (2004) 53-82. Survey; spectral/Cheeger flavour.
  bezrukov_1994_isoperimetric_discrete_spaces.pdf
                                           Bezrukov, Isoperimetric Problems in Discrete Spaces, Bolyai
                                           Soc. Math. Stud. 3 (1994) 59-91. Survey, cube-centred.
  prellberg_owczarek_1999_finite_perimeter_vesicles.pdf
                                           Prellberg & Owczarek, On the asymptotics of the finite-
                                           perimeter partition function of two-dimensional lattice
                                           vesicles, Commun. Math. Phys. 201(3) (1999) 493-505. Sieben's
                                           [10]. The BOND-perimeter twin of M(n) is their eq (3.1),
                                           stated in passing: max area of a polygon of perimeter 2n is
                                           n^2/4 (n even), (n^2-1)/4 (n odd) -- the 1/16 against our 1/8.

  sieben_2008_minimum_site_perimeter.pdf   Sieben, Polyominoes with minimum site-perimeter and full set
                                           achievement games, European J. Combin. 29(1) (2008) 108-117.
                                           Obtained 2026-08-06. Thm 4.1: sigma(e) = floor(e^2/8 - e/2 + 1)
                                           is M(n) verbatim; Thm 5.3: eps(s) = ceil(2 + sqrt(8s-4)).
                                           Connected animals, holes allowed, up to congruence.
  altshuler_etal_2006_minimal_perimeter_polyominoes.pdf
                                           Altshuler, Yanovsky, Vainsencher, Wagner & Bruckstein, On
                                           minimal perimeter polyminoes, DGCI 2006, LNCS 4245, 17-28.
                                           Obtained 2026-08-06. Same numbers (Thm 1) for an ARBITRARY
                                           finite subset of Z^2 -- no connectivity hypothesis, which is
                                           what closes our multi-hole case (union of all the holes).
                                           Thm 7 (sec 3.1) is a self-contained reproof; sec 3.2 does the
                                           8-connectivity metric.

Pattern theorems / rigorous animal statistics (obtained 2026-08-06 from the
citation scan of the isoperimetry batch):
  madras_1999_pattern_theorem_lattice_clusters.pdf
                                           Madras, A pattern theorem for lattice clusters, Ann. Combin.
                                           3 (1999) 357-384. LOAD-BEARING. Thm 2.1: clusters containing
                                           at most eps*n translates of a proper pattern P = (present
                                           sites, ABSENT sites) are exponentially rare. His Sec 3.1(f),
                                           the spread-out lattice of range M under the sup norm, IS the
                                           king lattice at M = 1, and site animals on it are covered.
                                           Consequences for us: lambda_0 < lambda for hole-free animals
                                           (pattern = 8 neighbours present, centre absent) is a corollary,
                                           not a measurement -- results/hole-free-growth-constant.md; and
                                           a(n+1)/a(n) -> lambda is a theorem (Thm 2.2 / Cor 3.6), which
                                           licenses the report's ratio fits. Madras never mentions holes.
  fisher_guttmann_whittington_1991_2d_vesicles_polygons.pdf
                                           Fisher, Guttmann & Whittington, Two-dimensional lattice
                                           vesicles and polygons, J. Phys. A 24 (1991) 3095-3106. The
                                           bond-perimeter maximum, p. 3097: max area is m^2/16 for
                                           perimeter m divisible by 4, else (m^2-4)/16 -- stated as
                                           "clearly", same as Prellberg-Owczarek's (3.1). Two sources,
                                           both calling it obvious: the 1/16 twin is folklore, with no
                                           primary to cite. Prove it in a line or attribute loosely.
  asinowski_etal_2012_polycubes_n_minus_3.pdf
                                           Asinowski, Barequet, Barequet & Rote, Proper n-cell polycubes
                                           in n-3 dimensions, J. Integer Seq. 15 (2012) #12.8.4. Open
                                           access. Completes the polycube defect-diagonal set with the
                                           explicit k=3 case.

Found by web search 2026-08-06, both free and both bearing on hole claims:
  soteros_whittington_1990_rigorous_results_wild_guesses.pdf
                                           Whittington & Soteros, Lattice animals: rigorous results and
                                           wild guesses, in Disorder in Physical Systems (Grimmett &
                                           Welsh, eds.), OUP 1990, 323-335. Free from Grimmett's own
                                           Hammersley-festschrift page at statslab.cam.ac.uk. NOTE the
                                           title page reads "S.G. Whittington and C.E. Soteros" -- the
                                           Barequet-Rote order is right, Madras's bibliography reversed it.
  guttmann_jensen_wong_enting_2000_punctured_polygons_polyominoes.pdf
                                           Guttmann, Jensen, Wong & Enting, Punctured polygons and
                                           polyominoes on the square lattice, J. Phys. A 33 (2000)
                                           1735-1764; arXiv cond-mat/0003441. Turned up while hunting the
                                           1990 "punctured discs" paper, and worth more: their Appendix
                                           proves that k-punctured polyominoes share the unpunctured
                                           growth constant and that the exponent shifts by exactly 1 per
                                           puncture (phi_k = phi_0 - k). That is the asymptotic content of
                                           our hole-fill bijection, for square-lattice polyominoes --
                                           results/hole-fill-interior-cell-identity.md now cites it. Their
                                           method is van Rensburg & Whittington's concatenation/drilling/
                                           surgery, which also largely discharges the want for that paper.
